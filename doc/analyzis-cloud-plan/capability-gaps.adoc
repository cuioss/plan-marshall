= Capability gaps: the concepts against the cloud lane
:toc: left
:toclevels: 3

The five reports measured what the cloud runs *produced*. This document works the other way: it takes
each capability plan-marshall documents in `doc/concepts/` and asks whether the cloud lane has an
equivalent, then looks for data on whether the absence mattered.

**The contract under analysis.** The cloud lane is defined by two project-local skills:
link:../../.claude/skills/cloud-plan-lane/SKILL.md[`cloud-plan-lane`], the execution contract loaded
as the first action of every run, and
link:../../.claude/skills/author-cloud-plan/SKILL.md[`author-cloud-plan`], the authoring judgement
applied when a plan is written. Every lane-contract finding below names one of them.

[IMPORTANT]
====
**This is not a marketing sheet, and it is deliberately structured to make that checkable.**

A capability plan-marshall has and the lane lacks is **not automatically a finding**. Every row below
carries an **evidence class**, and the classes are not interchangeable:

[cols="1,4",options="header"]
|===
| Class | Meaning

| **measured**
| The absence has a consequence quantified in one or more of the five reports.

| **observed**
| The absence produced at least one named incident, but nothing counts how often.

| **unexercised**
| The capability is absent and *nothing in the corpus tests whether that matters*. An unexercised gap
  is a statement about coverage, not about risk realised — and it is recorded as such rather than
  dressed up as a near-miss.
|===

**Three of the seven gaps below are `unexercised`.** They are the honest weak half of this document
and they are labelled, not buried. A section on what the cloud lane does *better*, and a section on where
plan-marshall's own machinery is measurably broken, follow the gap list — both are load-bearing, not
balance for its own sake.
====

== Method

Each of the 22 pages under `doc/concepts/` was read for the capability it claims, then checked against
`.claude/skills/cloud-plan-lane/SKILL.md` (2,266 lines) and `author-cloud-plan/SKILL.md` (233), which
together are the lane's complete contract — both project-local under `.claude/skills/`, which § 6 shows
is not incidental. Where an equivalent was absent, the five reports and the PR
record were searched for data.

**The lane's waivers are explicit, not accidental.** `CLAUDE.md` § Standalone Plan Lane names them in
a table: build commands bypass the architecture resolver, CI operations bypass the abstraction, `.plan/`
is untouched, and the Bash discipline does not bind. Every gap below follows from that table or from
the absence of `.plan/` in a fresh clone. **The lane was designed this way**, because its whole point
was to run without plan-marshall's machinery. Reading these as oversights would be reading the control
condition as a defect.

^m^ **`unexercised` classifies the *consequence*, not the mechanism.** For this row the mechanism is
directly verified in both contracts — unlike permission narrowing, where nothing is recorded at all.
What is missing is any corpus datum showing what the difference cost.

Two further capabilities — the **audit trail** (`audit-trail.adoc`) and **orchestration**
(`orchestration.adoc`) — are already results 1 and 3 in xref:README.adoc[the findings summary] and are
not restated here.

== 1. Untrusted-content ingestion — *unexercised*

`security.adoc` Layer 3 names the threat precisely, and names this surface among its examples: *"web
pages fetched during research, GitHub issue/PR/comment bodies fetched during CI/review, Sonar issue
messages fetched during triage. Any of these can carry a prompt-injection payload crafted to read as
instructions and hijack a write-capable LLM context."*

plan-marshall's containment is a three-role split — a read-only reader (`WebSearch, WebFetch, Read,
Grep`, no `Write`/`Edit`/`Bash`/`Skill`), a deterministic validator script
(`untrusted-ingestion:validate_struct`) that clamps the schema and checks the domain allowlist, and a
write-capable consumer that sees **only** the validated struct. ADR-003 records that the containment is
*the script*, not the read-only surface.

The cloud lane's § Step 7 instructs, of PR comment bodies from all three GitHub surfaces:

[quote]
____
Read the actual comment bodies … Handle **every** comment: fix it, or reply on the thread explaining
why it is not actionable.
____

That is third-party text — bot- and human-authored — read directly into a context holding `Write`,
`Edit` and `Bash`, with a contractual instruction to act on it. There is no reader isolation, no
validator, no schema clamp and no allowlist. **`untrusted`, `injection`, `sanitize` and `validate_struct`
appear zero times in the lane's 2,266 lines**; the only security-adjacent line is a table row mapping
*"security-relevant change"* to a persona skill.

[CAUTION]
====
**No incident was observed, and that is not evidence of safety.**

Nothing in the corpus indicates a comment body ever carried a payload, and this document does not
suggest one did. But the lane records nothing that *would* surface it: there is no findings store, no
provenance on ingested text, and the run report is written by the same context that read the comments.
An injection that succeeded would be indistinguishable, in the artifacts that exist, from the run
deciding to do something.

⇒ The honest statement is **coverage, not risk**: a surface plan-marshall isolates with a deterministic
gate is consumed raw here, and the corpus cannot say what that cost, because it never looked.
====

**One adjacent defect *was* measured, and it is the same root without the adversary.** All three
`multiplattform` run reports published reviewer counts that do not reproduce — 11 vs 10, "9 actionable"
vs 12, and "23 comments over 10 threads" vs 20 threads. The likely cause is that the reports
**transcribed CodeRabbit's own `Actionable comments posted: N` summary line** and republished it as a
derived figure. That is untrusted external text entering the record unvalidated — benign in intent,
wrong in fact, and **neither report names which instrument it used**.

== 2. Deterministic behaviour through scripts — *measured*

`security.adoc` Layer 2 and `tools-and-scripts.adoc` describe one funnel: `python3
.plan/execute-script.py {bundle}:{skill}:{script}`, a Python proxy with an embedded notation map that
never `exec`s a shell string, resolves only registered scripts, and logs every call to
`script-execution.log`. Its effect on *behaviour* is that a figure is produced by a program rather than
by a model, and the program can be re-run.

The lane cannot use it: the executor is generated and git-ignored, so a fresh clone has none. Every
figure a cloud run publishes is produced by the model reading the tree, and `CLAUDE.md` explicitly
restores ordinary shell use in the lane, including loops and `$()`.

The consequence is measured three ways:

* **F11 — *"require every count in a report to carry the command that produced it"* — is recorded as
  `nearly universal`** across `code-intelligence-substrate`'s 37 plans. Not an occasional lapse: the
  default.
* **`test-quality`'s plan `100` shipped four false figures**, each wrong in the *comparison* rather
  than in either number — precisely the class a re-run catches and a re-read does not.
* **The three unreproducible reviewer counts above.**

**The reference report's strongest single finding is the positive control for this.** In plan `500`,
*every* mechanically-derived census figure re-derived exactly while **five** asserted narrative
sections had gone stale. Same run, same agent, same day — **derivation held; assertion did not.** That
is as close to a controlled comparison as this corpus offers, and it is internal to the cloud lane,
so it is not confounded by any lane difference at all.

== 3. Mechanical state gates — *measured*

`process-enforcement.adoc` Layer 4 is the phase handshake: 14 invariants fingerprinted at each phase
boundary and re-evaluated at the next phase's entry, refusing to advance on drift. Its stated purpose is
exactly the distinction at issue — *"the difference between **the LLM said it edited 5 files** and **the
worktree shows 5 modified files at the expected SHA**."*

The lane has no equivalent, and **its own contract concedes it**. § Step 9 requires the run to re-verify
its report's filesystem claims by re-reading them, and explains why:

[quote]
____
No gate can catch this, because the claim is about the filesystem rather than the code: the suite stays
green while the sentence goes false.
____

The contract then records **two observed failures of that manual remedy**: a report that described
`.plan/` accurately when written and falsely by the time the build gate had run, and a run that rebased
nine commits and left two documents citing SHAs *"that existed on no branch under review"*.

⇒ **The most-replicated finding in the whole document set is this gap's measured cost.** Gaps that
correct the run's own report: **16.1%**, **16.3%**, **18.1%** — three corpora, three briefs, 2.0 points
apart.

**The lane's remedy is a diligence instruction on the party being checked.** § Step 9's compliance
audit is performed by the agent whose compliance is in question, which is the self-assessment defect one
level up. That it catches anything at all is a point in its favour; that it is the only mechanism is the
gap.

== 4. Findings store with a blocking gate — *measured*

plan-marshall's findings are append-only JSONL, hash-id deduplicated, typed by domain
(`pr-comment`, `sonar-issue`, `build-error`, `qgate-*`), carrying resolution fields — and **the next
phase's entry protocol refuses to start until every pending finding has a resolution**
(`process-enforcement.adoc` Layer 3).

The lane's equivalent is a `## Findings` section in a markdown report. In fairness it is a *carefully
designed* section — it requires a source, a description and a disposition from a closed four-value set
(`fixed` / `rejected-with-reason` / `deferred` / `survivor`), plus a stop record naming which exit ended
the verification loop. As a contract it is better specified than most machine formats.

**But nothing reads it.** It is prose, written by the agent being audited, queried by no gate. The
measured consequence is a merge state that is structurally unreachable in the local lane:

* `test-quality` — **5 of 21** reviewer findings merged with the thread unclosed.
* `multiplattform` — **5 of 42**.
* `truthful-signals` — **57.1%** resolution against the local lane's **100%**.

**Four of the five reports independently recommend "give the lane a blocking findings gate."** They
converged on it without naming the mechanism that already exists one lane over.

== 5. Worktree isolation and locking — *observed*

`parallelism-and-locking.adoc` describes per-plan worktrees plus two file-based locks: a merge mutex
using an `O_EXCL` atomic create with plan-liveness reclamation, and a machine-global build-queue limiter
with a self-healing stale reaper folded into the same atomic read-modify-write.

The cloud lane dispatches sub-agents — a verification sub-agent, mutation sweeps — into **one** working
tree, with nothing serializing them. `truthful-signals` § Adjudication records the incident:

[quote]
____
Nothing governs many agents in one working tree. The audit run hit this itself — agents mutation-testing
concurrently corrupted each other's snapshots. Fixed in-flight; the contract still carries no rule.
____

**This has since been partly closed, and the shape of the fix is the point.** The lane contract now
requires per-agent scratch paths (`$TMPDIR/{agent}-mutsweep/…`, *"never a shared generic one"*) and a
`git status` re-check after each sweep. That is a **naming convention plus a diligence check**, where
plan-marshall's answer to the same hazard is an atomic filesystem primitive with crash reclamation. Both
address the incident; they are not in the same robustness class, and only one of them survives an agent
that does not follow instructions.

⇒ Recorded as `observed` rather than `measured`: one named incident, self-reported, with no count of how
often it recurred.

== 6. Deterministic skill injection — *unexercised consequence, verified mechanism*

`skill-handling.adoc` opens by describing, almost exactly, the condition the cloud lane runs in:

[quote]
____
In an unconstrained Claude Code session, "which skill applies here" is a runtime guess the model makes
from whatever the prompt happened to surface. Two tasks with similar wording can pull in different skill
sets and produce divergent outputs. The skill load isn't even logged in a way that lets you check after
the fact.
____

plan-marshall's answer is a data flow with no inference in it: `skills_by_profile` is recorded at
architecture-enrichment time, materialised into `task.skills` at plan time, and issued as one `Skill:`
load per entry by the dispatcher — *"the LLM running the subagent has **zero discretion** about its own
context — it loads what it is told."* Each load lands in `work.log` under a `[SKILL]` tag, and the
concept states the audit rule plainly: **if a skill is missing from the log, it never loaded, regardless
of what the workflow body claims to have applied.**

The lane's § Step 1 is the other design. Four differences, each read directly out of the two contracts:

=== (a) Selection is the agent's judgement

The lane instructs: *"Load the work identity, then **only the domain skills the plan's surface actually
needs**."* The mechanism is a **seven-row conditional table** — Python production code, Python tests,
`SKILL.md` structure, `.adoc` docs, security-relevant change, and so on — applied by the agent reading
its own plan. That is the runtime guess `skill-handling.adoc` declines to make, reintroduced as a
documented procedure. Two plans touching similar surfaces can load different sets, and nothing
reconciles them.

=== (b) Tier 1 is absent

`persona-plan-marshall-agent` is *the* Tier-1 skill — loaded unconditionally by the dispatcher on every
plan-marshall dispatch, carrying the hard rules that are Layer 1 of **both** `security.adoc` and
`process-enforcement.adoc`.

The lane's **Always** list has two entries, and it is not among them: `ref-code-quality` and
`plugin-script-architecture`. Nor is it in the conditional table.

⇒ This is direct confirmation of something the rest of this document inferred from `CLAUDE.md`'s waiver
table. **Layer 1 is not weakened in the cloud lane; it is never loaded.** The hard rules the concept
pages describe as *"in the LLM's context window every time it dispatches"* are, here, in no context at
all.

=== (c) `Read` is not a skill load, and personas are the case where it matters

The lane's primary route is `Read: marketplace/bundles/{bundle}/skills/{skill}/SKILL.md`. For a
standards document that is close to equivalent — the text lands in context either way.

For a **persona** it is not, and the gap is structural. A persona is a shell that *declares* a
composition DAG which `manage-personas resolve` flattens into a deduped `skills[]`. `persona-implementer`
resolves to its base `persona-plan-marshall-agent`, plus `ref-code-quality`, plus the profile × domain
skills for `implementation` and `quality`. The file itself says it *"carries no executable workflow of
its own."*

⇒ A lane run that `Read`s `persona-implementer/SKILL.md` obtains **a pointer document describing a
composition it cannot perform**. The base never arrives, and neither does the profile × domain merge —
because the resolver is a script, and the lane has no executor. The lane hand-approximates exactly one
edge of that DAG by listing `ref-code-quality` in its Always table; the base and the domain merge have no
substitute.

=== (d) What loaded is self-reported

`skill-handling.adoc` makes the work log authoritative over any claim. The lane's § Step 9 compliance
table records the artifact proving Step 1 as: **"Named in the report."**

⇒ The account of what context the run had is written by the run, from memory, with nothing to check it
against — the self-assessment defect of § 3 applied to the run's own context. A skill named in the report
and never loaded is indistinguishable from one that was.

[CAUTION]
====
**The lane escapes the worst of this by a coincidence of subject matter, and its own contract says so.**

Step 1's `Read`-the-bundle-path route works because *"this repository **is** the marketplace, so every
skill named below is a file in the tree."* The contract also records the environmental fact behind that
workaround: *"the `plan-marshall` plugin is frequently **not** installed in a Claude Code cloud session —
this has been observed, with the plugin cache verified absent."*

⇒ **In any other project, a cloud-lane run would have `.claude/skills/` and nothing else.** No bundle
tree to read, no plugin cache to load from — so the seven-row table, the two Always entries and the
persona shells would all be unreachable, and the lane would run on project-local skills alone. The
capability measured in these five reports is therefore the **best case** for the lane, available only
because the subject under test happened to be the marketplace itself.
====

**No corpus datum measures what any of this cost.** Nothing in the five reports attributes a defect to
a skill that did not load — which is unsurprising, since the only record of what loaded is the run's own
sentence about it. Classified `unexercised` on consequence for that reason, while the mechanism above is
verified line by line in both contracts.

== 7. Permission narrowing — *unexercised*

`security.adoc` Layers 1–2 argue that one broad allow rule over the executor proxy replaces either a
prompt per operation or a long, error-prone allowlist — and that prompts which *do* fire are a signal
that a hard rule leaked.

A cloud session has no executor, and the lane restores ordinary shell use by contract. What permission
posture the cloud VM actually ran under is **not recorded in any corpus**, and no report measures blast
radius, prompt frequency, or a single denied action.

⇒ **This row exists to mark an absence of measurement, not to imply a problem.** It is the one gap
where this document has neither a mechanism trace nor an incident — only the observation that a control
condition ran for two weeks and nothing captured its permission behaviour.

== What the cloud lane does better

Excluding these would make the document dishonest, and one of them is a mechanism plan-marshall does not
have in the same form.

[CAUTION]
====
**Correction — this section originally claimed two mechanisms as cloud-lane inventions. Both are
present in plan-marshall, and one of them is better specified there.**

The original text read *"the bounded adversarial verification loop … is a cloud-lane invention
plan-marshall does not have in the same form"* and *"the `non-converging` signal … is a quality signal
plan-marshall does not emit."* Both were refuted against
`phase-6-finalize/workflow/pre-submission-self-review.md`.

**The error is this document's own archetype, and the cause is stated in § Method.** That section
declares the analysis was drawn against `doc/concepts/` and the lane contract. The self-review loop
appears in `doc/concepts/` only in passing — one clause in `automatic-reviews.adoc`, one table cell in
`planning-workflow.adoc`, and `verification.adoc` does not mention it at all — so the absence was real
*within the scope searched*, and the claim was then phrased about plan-marshall, which is wider.

That is precisely what `pre-submission-self-review.md` § "Absence claims state the scope they were drawn
against" forbids: *"a round asserted a literal appeared in 'zero test and source files' while three
survivors sat in merged `main`, because the sweep was scoped to one directory tree while the CLAIM said
'test and source' — the claim was wider than the scope searched."* Recorded here rather than silently
fixed, per the conventions.
====

What the lane genuinely has that plan-marshall does not is **narrower than two mechanisms — it is two
properties of one**:

* **Verifier independence, unconditionally.** The lane dispatches a sub-agent that *"has not seen
  this plan or the diff."* plan-marshall dispatches its cognitive phase to a fresh
  `execution-context` leaf with `skills: []` and nothing but the candidate envelope — genuinely
  independent — **but only above a candidate-count gate**. Below the threshold the identical review runs
  *inline in the dispatcher's context*, and the contract calls that gate *"a pure dispatch-cost
  optimization — semantics are preserved bit-for-bit."* **The return-TOON semantics are preserved;
  the independence is not.** That is the sharper finding, and neither this document's original claim nor
  the refutation of it had it: the cheap branch trades away the reviewer's independence under a heading
  that says nothing is being traded.
* **The stop question is put TO the verifier.** The lane asks it directly — *"does anything you
  found remain that condition A or B forbids leaving open?"* — with A and B quoted, every prior-round
  survivor listed, and the contract stating it *"is asked of the verifier here rather than decided by
  the author afterwards."* plan-marshall closes on a predicate over the review's return instead: a
  full-surface clean pass, mechanically evaluated. That is the review's own verdict rather than the
  author's assertion, so the gap is narrower than *"self-assessed versus adversarially assessed"* — but
  no question is put, and no survivor list is re-put to anyone.
* **The `non-converging` reporting position.** The lane puts the exit in the **report header**, where a
  collector reads it. plan-marshall's equivalent is a `manage-logging decision --level WARNING`. Both
  distinguish the two closes; the lane makes the distinction harder to miss.

**What is NOT a gap**, having been checked:

[cols="3,4",options="header"]
|===
| Mechanism | Where it stands

| Bounded multi-round loop
| **Present and richer.** `head_dependent: true` re-fires every loop-back, `--since-ref` anchors each
  round's delta, and only a full-surface clean pass may record `done`.

| The converged-vs-out-of-budget distinction
| **Present and named**, at § "Round-loop termination": *"These are two DIFFERENT closes and a later
  reader MUST NOT collapse them."* The original claim here was simply wrong.

| The self-seeding class
| **Present, and absent from the lane.** plan-marshall names why the doc-claim half does not converge
  under correction — *"correction breeds the next instance of the class; only DELETION converges"* — and
  requires resolution by deletion.

| The n−1-of-n remedy
| **Present, and the lane only diagnoses it.** The lane observes that *"a fix applied at n−1 of n sites
  is why a corrected claim keeps reappearing."* plan-marshall encodes the cure: a check firing with
  `defect_class` D must re-scan every other surfaced candidate for D and file every member in the same
  round, with `cohort_size` on each finding so *"a cohort of one is distinguishable from a cohort whose
  remaining members were never looked for."*
|===

The lane's other genuine strengths stand unchanged:

* **Evidence stronger than a read.** The stopping round must name a differential run, a fuzz sweep, a
  mutation campaign or a branch enumeration — not a re-reading.
* **Throughput.** 51 runs across five epics in the measured week, without an orchestrator session held
  open.
* **The stop record and the survivor rows.** Requiring every survivor to be individually listed, proved
  equivalent or explicitly bounded, and *re-put to the verifier in the stopping round*, is stricter than
  anything in the local lane's finalize path.

== Where plan-marshall's own machinery is measurably broken

The concept pages are the *claim*. Three of them are contradicted by data in this repository, and the
reports that found it did so against plan-marshall, not against the lane.

* **The audit trail has holes.** `PLAN-TRUTH-074`'s retrospective found the change-ledger held **0 build
  rows while 144 `pyproject_build` calls ran**, and all 25 dispatch-boundary rows carried unpopulated
  context-load columns — so the billing column was empty for every phase. `audit-trail.adoc` calls the
  trail *"exhaustive"*. On that plan it was not.
* **Every enforcement layer is explicitly not enforcement.** Both `security.adoc` and
  `process-enforcement.adoc` carry a `CAUTION` saying so: Layer 1 is skill-loaded text the LLM can
  ignore, Layer 3 depends on the LLM invoking the query, and Layer 4 depends on the orchestrator running
  the verify at the right moment. **The documents are honest about this**; a comparison that quotes the
  capability without the caveat would not be.
* **The self-review loop is essentially undocumented at concept level, and that produced a false
  finding in this very document.** `pre-submission-self-review.md` is 432 lines carrying the bounded
  round loop, the delta anchor, the converged-vs-out-of-budget termination criterion, the self-seeding
  class and the cohort sweep. In `doc/concepts/` — the entry point a reader or an agent starts from —
  it survives as **one clause** in `automatic-reviews.adoc`, **one table cell** in
  `planning-workflow.adoc`, and nothing in `verification.adoc`, the page whose title promises it.
  ⇒ An analysis scoped to `doc/concepts/` cannot see the mechanism, and this document's first draft
  duly concluded it did not exist. **The documentation gap is not cosmetic: it manufactured a false
  negative about plan-marshall in an audit of plan-marshall.**
* **The gap is instrument depth as well as lane quality.** The cloud pass checks every deliverable on
  four axes and sweeps adjacent surfaces; the local check examines the claims the plan actually made.
  Part of every difference in this document set is the instrument, not the lane — stated in the method
  and restated here because it applies to this document too.

== What this document does not establish

. **No gap here is quantified as a cost.** The five reports measure outcomes; this document traces
  outcomes to absent mechanisms. **A trace is an argument, not a measurement** — nothing establishes
  that adding a handshake would have moved the 16.1% report-correction rate to zero.
. **Three of seven gaps have no consequence data** (`unexercised`), and one of those is the security gap
  that motivated this analysis. That is the finding: **the corpus cannot speak to it**, in either direction.
. **The lane was built to lack these things.** It is the control condition. A control that lacks the
  treatment is working correctly.
. **No causal claim survives the effort confound.** Cloud runs used Opus at max effort; local runs use
  per-phase resolved effort. On quality this cuts *toward* the conclusion — the more expensive setting
  still produced these figures — but it is a confound either way.
