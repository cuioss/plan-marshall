# Step 3c review-gate outline re-dispatch is blocked by its own entry handshake when q_gate_validation=once leaves findings pending

## Problem

Under `plan.phase-3-outline.q_gate_validation: once` (the default), `planning-outline.md` § Step 2b states:

> **`once`** (default) — the FIRST-pass q-gate-validation dispatch already ran. Do NOT auto-loop... Any remaining `qgate_pending_count > 0` findings surface to the operator at the Step 3 user-review gate rather than being auto-fixed.

Step 3c then documents the remedy for that gate: capture the operator's change requests and *"Re-dispatch phase-3-outline ONCE via the same `Task: plan-marshall:{target}` envelope used in Step 2"*.

That re-dispatch cannot run. `phase-3-outline` SKILL.md:115 executes the Phase Entry Protocol's `phase_handshake verify --phase 2-refine --strict` before any phase work, and the captured `2-refine` invariant row includes `pending_findings_blocking_count`. The q-gate findings that *caused* the operator to be consulted are exactly what makes that value diverge from its captured baseline, so the entry verify returns `status: drift` and the leaf stops before reading the findings it was dispatched to fix.

**The documented recovery path is blocked by the state it exists to recover from.**

## Evidence

Observed 2026-09-02 on plan `dual-homed-hook-install-renders-identically`. The `3-outline` q-gate pass filed two `error`-severity findings. Pre-checking the verify the re-dispatch would run:

```text
$ phase_handshake verify --plan-id ... --phase 2-refine --strict
status: drift
drift_count: 1
diffs[1]{invariant,captured,observed}:
  pending_findings_blocking_count,"0","2"
```

Exit code 1. A bare re-dispatch would have burned a full level-5 envelope to stop at its first step.

Note the asymmetry that makes this specific to the `once` path: `until_clean` re-dispatches phase-3-outline from *inside* the Step 2b auto-loop, where the same drift exists — so the defect is not unique to the operator gate, it is simply most visible there because `once` is the default.

## Workaround used

`phase_handshake capture --phase 2-refine --override --reason "..."` to re-baseline the row before dispatching. This is the sanctioned override surface and it worked, but it is undocumented at this site: nothing in Step 3c or Step 2b tells the orchestrator that the re-dispatch needs it, so the default behaviour is a wasted envelope and a confusing drift halt.

## Directive

Decide which of the two is right and make the docs and the invariant agree — do not paper over it with an override the caller has to discover by trial:

- If a pending q-gate finding is legitimately *not* drift at a re-entry (it is the phase's input, not a regression), then `pending_findings_blocking_count` should not participate in the phases-2-6 entry verify, or the re-entry should verify against a re-entry-specific row.
- If it is drift, then Step 2b (`once` and `until_clean` alike) and Step 3c must document the `capture --override --reason` re-baseline as a required step of the re-dispatch, with the reason text it should carry.

Whichever way it resolves, check the sibling site: `planning-outline.md` § Step 4's phase-4-plan `until_clean` loop re-dispatches under the same conditions against a captured `3-outline` row, and `planning.md` § Action: init's phase-2-refine `refine_prompt` re-dispatch re-enters against a captured `1-init` row. Derive that set from an enumeration of re-dispatch sites, not from this list — these three are the ones this run happened to traverse, not a verified total.

## The drift fires in BOTH directions — confirmed in the same run

The framing above ("pending findings block the re-entry") is only half of it. After the Step 3c re-dispatch cleared both findings, the NEXT boundary drifted too, in the opposite direction:

```text
$ phase_handshake verify --plan-id ... --phase 3-outline --strict
status: drift
drift_count: 2
diffs[2]{invariant,captured,observed}:
  qgate_open_count,"2","0"
  pending_findings_blocking_count,"2","0"
```

The `3-outline` row was captured at the Step 2c boundary while the two findings were still pending, so *resolving them* — the entire point of the review gate — is reported as drift when Step 4 verifies that row before dispatching phase-4-plan. A second `capture --override` was needed.

So one review-gate cycle that works exactly as designed requires **two** overrides: one to enter the re-dispatch, one to leave it. Any fix must handle both directions; suppressing only the pending-side drift would leave the resolved-side halt in place.
