title: CodeRabbit rate-limit ETA phrasing not matched by rate_limit_eta_patterns
content: "## Problem

CodeRabbit refused a re-review with a concrete ETA in its in-place summary edit:

> **Review limit reached** — Next included review available in **27 minutes**.

The registry RECOGNISED the refusal (classified `layer: registry_refusal_patterns`, `refusal_class: awaitable_window`, `refusal_cause: quota`, `window_claim_made: true`) but returned `refusal_eta: \"\"`. The `rate_limit_eta_patterns` for coderabbit in `automatic-review/standards/coderabbit.md` do not match this phrasing.

## Why it costs real time

Recognition without extraction turns a **targeted ~27-minute wait** into a **blind >=90-minute one**. On a plan whose operator posture is 10 permitted waits, that is the difference between one short wait and burning hours of wall-clock across a run — and the information needed to avoid it was present, in the comment, and discarded at the parse.

The asymmetry is worth stating plainly: the refusal-recognition patterns and the ETA-extraction patterns are maintained as if they were one surface, but they fail independently. Recognition succeeding is not evidence extraction did.

## Directive

1. Add the observed phrasing to coderabbit's `rate_limit_eta_patterns`. The literal seen was `Next included review available in 27 minutes` inside a `**Review limit reached**` block; write the pattern against the minutes-quantity shape, not the exact count.
2. Publish extraction as a FIRST-CLASS field distinct from recognition, so a refusal that was recognised but whose ETA was not extracted is visible rather than indistinguishable from one that stated no ETA at all. `window_claim_made: true` with an empty `refusal_eta` is exactly that state and currently reads as \"the bot gave no ETA\".
3. Add a test pinning the observed literal against the pattern set, so a future phrasing change fails the suite instead of silently reverting to blind waits.

## Also observed in the same run

`cuioss-review-bot` (a REQUIRED bot) had produced no output newer than the Guide and \"No code suggestions found\" from before the previous HEAD. Its silence is not participation for the current HEAD; a re-dispatch after the CodeRabbit window reopens needs its own trigger. That is a separate concern from this defect but was invisible until the raw comment fetch was read directly — the participation guard was never reached, because the `ask` branch returns first.
