# A scope addition at the outline Step 3c review gate never re-runs the component-assessment pass, so every late-added file arrives at 4-plan missing its CERTAIN_INCLUDE

**Proposed component**: `plan-marshall:phase-3-outline`
**Proposed category**: `bug`
**Evidence class**: Q-Gate findings raised AND resolved in-run (the slipped-then-caught class)
**Signal**: `signal_qgate_pending_count` — all 3 of the run's 4-plan Q-Gate findings are instances, at a 100% reproduction rate

## The observation

Three files were added to deliverable 2's Affected files at the phase-3-outline Step 3c review gate, as the remediation for three outline Q-Gate findings:

| File | Added by 3-outline finding | Produced 4-plan finding |
|------|---------------------------|-------------------------|
| `manage-config/standards/api-reference.md` | `addc9a` | `43d9b5` |
| `manage-config/standards/provisioning-fail-closed-audit.md` | `7f3191` | `0679c0` |
| `doc/user/configuration.adoc` | `9b058b` (operator override) | `7200e8` |

**All three, without exception, produced a 4-plan Q-Gate finding of the identical shape**: "Deliverable 2 lists {path} in Affected files, but no CERTAIN_INCLUDE assessment exists for this path." All three were resolved by a backfill whose resolution detail states the same cause verbatim: "The coverage gap was a bookkeeping artifact: the file was added to D2 at the Step 3c review gate, after the original assessment pass had already run."

Three additions, three findings, three identical diagnoses. This is not a judgement failure that happened to recur — it is a deterministic mechanical consequence of step ordering, and it will fire once per late-added file on every plan that remediates an under-coverage finding at 3c.

## The mechanism

The component-assessment pass runs once, over the deliverable set as it stands at that moment. The Step 3c review gate then *mutates* that deliverable set — adding files is precisely what remediating an `under_coverage` finding means. Nothing re-runs the assessment over the delta, and nothing at the 3-outline exit checks that the assessment set still covers the Affected-files set. The mismatch is therefore invisible until phase-4-plan's Q-Gate re-derives coverage from scratch and reports it as a fresh finding.

The two phases disagree about who owns the invariant: 3-outline treats the assessment as a completed pass, 4-plan treats it as a precondition. Nobody re-establishes it across the mutation in between.

## Why the current outcome is worse than it looks

Each instance costs a full Q-Gate round trip — detection, an agent-authored finding, a triage decision, a backfill call, a resolution record — to restore a fact that was fully determined the moment the file was added. Every one of the three backfills recorded `confidence 100`, because there was never any doubt: the outline had *just* argued the file into scope, in writing, with reasons. The information needed for the assessment existed at the moment of the addition and was simply not written down.

It also actively degrades the signal: three of this run's eleven Q-Gate findings are pure bookkeeping noise with no defect behind them, which inflates the very count that gates whether this lessons-capture step runs at all.

## Corrective rule (the actionable part)

Make the assessment obligation part of the scope addition rather than a separate pass that ran earlier:

1. **At Step 3c, adding a path to a deliverable's Affected files SHOULD carry its CERTAIN_INCLUDE assessment in the same action.** The gate that admits the file has, by construction, just produced the justification the assessment needs — `addc9a`'s remediation already wrote three paragraphs of reasoning about why api-reference.md belongs in D2. Recording a `confidence 100` assessment alongside it is bookkeeping, not analysis.
2. **Failing that, add a coverage reconciliation at the 3-outline exit** — assessed-set vs Affected-files-set, symmetric difference — so the gap is closed in the phase that created it rather than reported by the next one. Note the codebase already has this reconciliation shape for a sibling problem: `manage-references` performs a three-way declared/derived/realized footprint reconciliation by symmetric difference. The pattern is available; it is simply not applied at this seam.

Option 1 is preferred: it removes the window entirely, whereas option 2 only shortens it.

## Why this is an epic-level candidate rather than a plan-local note

The fix touches the phase-3-outline / phase-4-plan handoff contract, not this plan's subject matter, and the choice between the two remedies above is a workflow-architecture decision with blast radius across every plan that runs the outline review gate. `operator-ux` is the epic holding the surrounding phase-workflow work, so the routing decision belongs there.
