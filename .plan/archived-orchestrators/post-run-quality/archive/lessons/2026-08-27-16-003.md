# Verify a defect claim handed to the retrospective before reporting it as a finding

component: plan-marshall:plan-retrospective
category: anti-pattern
confidence: medium
source_plan: a-failing-ci-call-reports-success
source_pr: 1356

## Context

The dispatch context for this retrospective asserted a live defect: that the
lessons corpus was filing title-only stubs with empty bodies — "5 active lessons,
every one body-less, set-body never called after add".

A census refuted it. `manage-lessons list --status active` returned **6** lessons,
and reading every one of the six with `manage-lessons get` found every one
carrying a full multi-section body (Problem / Observed / Directive / Archetype,
typically 1.5-2.5 KB). Zero were body-less. The corpus count was also stale by one:
`2026-08-27-14-001` was filed after the dispatch context was composed.

## Root cause

A claim arriving through the finalize dispatch context carries the authority of
the run that produced it, and a retrospective that treats its inputs as facts will
restate it. Here the claim was cheap to check — one list call and six gets — and
was wrong in both its universal ("every one") and its count ("5").

## Proposed action

Treat a defect claim supplied in the dispatch context as a hypothesis with a named
verification, not as a finding. Where the claim is a set property over a store the
retrospective can read, derive it: list the population, read each member, report
the derived count. Had this one been restated, it would have filed a fabricated bug
against `manage-lessons` that read as corroborated because of where it came from.

## Evidence

- `manage-lessons list --status active` → total 6, filtered 6
- `manage-lessons get` on 2026-08-27-07-001, -07-002, -09-001, -12-001, -12-002, -14-001 → 6 of 6 with full bodies
- Related standing rule: derive completeness, never assert it
