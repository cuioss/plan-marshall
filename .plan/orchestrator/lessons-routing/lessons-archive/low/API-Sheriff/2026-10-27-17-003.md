# Outline claims made without a search: IT route exclusivity and recorded-open boundaries


# Two claims an outline makes without a search: "this IT route is exclusive to one suite" and "no other document records this boundary"

Source: PLAN-V02-05 (#526), candidate lessons 007 and 008; Q-Gate records c63827, 66f9aa and f5b64d.

## Observation

- The outline picked `/proxy/untrusted` for a new 5xx probe on the premise that no other suite dials its circuit-breaker key. `HtmlErrorPageIT` dials it concurrently on the same instance and `UpstreamHostnameVerificationIT` on another, so the probe could receive the breaker's 503 instead of 502.
- A deliverable closed the forward-all `not_modified` asymmetry, but ADR-0008 (Decision bullet and Amendment A1) still recorded that boundary as open, and the threat model's response-path description and information-disclosure entry were in no deliverable. Amendments in older ADRs are the easiest to miss.
- Superseding an ADR (PLAN-V02-09, ADR-0027 by ADR-0074) left two other records standing on the old text: ADR-0050 said 0027 was "Not superseded", and ADR-0032 section 5 said the exclusion removes the extension's health and metrics beans. The 4-plan Q-Gate caught both; the spec's affected-files list named neither.

## Rule

- Before claiming an IT route is exclusive to one suite, search every IT for the path. A breaker-keyed route shared across concurrent suites must tolerate the gateway's open-circuit 503 with its full signature (Retry-After, upstream problem type) and still fail a bare 503.
- When a plan closes a gap or adds a response-path control, search the ADR corpus and the threat model for every place that records the gap or describes that path, and give each hit to a documentation deliverable.
- When a spec supersedes an ADR or changes the behaviour one describes, search `doc/` for the superseded record's number and for the mechanism's key names, and list every hit as an affected file or document the exclusion.

## Apply

The outline of any plan that adds an IT probe route or closes a recorded-open boundary.
