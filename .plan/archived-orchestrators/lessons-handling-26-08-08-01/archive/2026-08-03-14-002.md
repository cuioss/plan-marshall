# Re-review that caught 14 unseen findings fired incidentally, not by design

## Context

On PR #1084 the first `automatic-review` pass completed green. The step's own warning line recorded what "green" actually meant:

```text
Only pr-agent reviewed PR #1084. coderabbit refused (awaitable_window) and sourcery refused
(hard_quota), review_rate_window_await=false so no recovery attempted. The green participation
verdict proves participation, not review coverage - 2 of 3 configured reviewers never saw this diff.
```

Roughly fifty minutes later, a `head_dependent` re-fire ran because an *unrelated* triage fix (PR-Agent finding `1a94d5`, committed at `79f379f56`) moved HEAD. That re-fire produced:

```text
The head_dependent re-fire caught a late reviewer: coderabbit, which refused with awaitable_window
on the first pass, delivered a full inline review of the branch and filed 14 findings - while
pr-agent simultaneously WITHDREW its own finding and now reports no major issues. A single green
participation verdict at the first HEAD would have merged this branch with 14 unseen comments.
```

## Root cause

Two independent facts combined:

1. `review_rate_window_await: false` in the plan's manifest meant a *recoverable* refusal (`awaitable_window` — a bot that will review later) received no wait and no retry. The refusal was recorded and then dropped.
2. Nothing else in the pipeline schedules a second look. The re-fire that rescued the run is keyed on HEAD movement, so it is a function of whether more code changed — not of whether coverage was ever achieved.

The safety therefore came from a coincidence. Had `pre-submission-self-review` ended cleanly with no triage fix to commit, HEAD would not have moved, no re-fire would have occurred, and the branch would have merged with 14 unreviewed findings including two Major.

This is distinct from lesson `2026-07-28-19-002` (participation must be derived from review CONTENT). That lesson says the *verdict* was wrong. This one says that even with a correct verdict, the pipeline has no designed path from "a recoverable refusal was observed" to "a second attempt happens".

## Proposed action

1. Make a recoverable refusal (`awaitable_window`) a first-class deferred obligation rather than a logged fact: record it on plan state and require it to be discharged — by a later successful review or by an explicit operator waiver — before `branch-cleanup` may merge.
2. Decouple the re-review trigger from HEAD movement. A re-review owed because of an unresolved refusal must fire whether or not any further commit lands.
3. When `review_rate_window_await` is `false` and a recoverable refusal occurs, say so at merge time, in the merge gate's own output — not only in the review step's warning line, which the merge gate does not read.
4. Distinguish recoverable from unrecoverable refusals at the config layer. Sourcery's `hard_quota` (size-keyed, per lesson `2026-07-28-23-002`) genuinely cannot be waited out; CodeRabbit's `awaitable_window` demonstrably could — it delivered 14 findings 50 minutes later.
