# A marshalld timeout verdict abandons the wait but never reaps the build, so every documented re-run compounds the load that caused it

## Problem

When a marshalld-routed build exceeds its budget, the client returns

```text
status: timeout
exit_code: -1
duration_seconds: 1800
error: timeout
timeout_used_seconds: 1800
```

and the caller proceeds. **The build itself keeps running.** The daemon abandons the wait; nothing reaps the child. `phase-5-execute`'s orchestrator-tier table then instructs the caller to *"Re-run the step once"* on a `timeout` — so the documented remedy launches a second full suite alongside the first, on the same cores.

This compounds. Each retry adds another concurrent suite, every suite gets slower, and each one is therefore more likely to time out and trigger another retry. The contract's own escalation ladder drives the machine further into the condition it is trying to diagnose.

## Evidence

Observed 2026-09-02 during finalize for `dual-homed-hook-install-renders-identically`, whose `module-tests plan-marshall` gate arm went:

| Attempt | Outcome | Wall |
|---|---|---|
| baseline (phase-5, clean machine) | success, 19526 passed | **362s** |
| 1 | timeout | 955s |
| 2 | harness-killed (no verdict) | — |
| 3 | **error**, 1 test failed on a 30s subprocess budget | 1381s (tests 1148s) |
| 4 | timeout | 1800s |

At attempt 4 a process scan for the worktree found **20 live processes**: a `build.py module-tests` from 30 minutes earlier, a second pytest controller with 10 xdist workers from 26 minutes earlier, and a true orphan re-parented to init. Two entire suites were still executing while a third ran.

After SIGTERM to the roots, the 10 xdist workers **survived and re-parented to init** — a plain kill of the controller does not take the pool down; the workers need an explicit second pass.

## The damage is not only slowness — it corrupts diagnosis

Attempt 3's single failure was `test_module_subcommand_accepts_canonical_module`, a `subprocess.TimeoutExpired` against a 30s budget. Measuring the underlying verb *on that loaded machine* produced 24-57s, which reads as a marginal-budget defect in `manage-architecture`. It was largely contention I had created by retrying.

⛔ **A latency measured while an abandoned build is still running is not a measurement of anything.** Before attributing slowness to code, scan for orphaned builds from your own worktree and reap them — otherwise the retry ladder manufactures the evidence that justifies the next retry.

## Directive

**The reap is the missing half of the timeout verdict.** A `timeout` return should either kill the process group it gave up on, or report the surviving PID so the caller can. Decide which, and make the `timeout` payload say what happened to the child — a verdict that reports only the wait's fate, while the work continues invisibly, is a status that describes half the system.

**The re-run instruction is unsafe until the reap exists.** Wherever a `timeout` is documented as "re-run once" (`phase-5-execute` § orchestrator-tier verification is the one this run traversed — derive the rest rather than trusting that list), the re-run must be preceded by a reap, or the instruction should be to reap-then-rerun.

**Killing the controller is not enough.** Any reap must handle the xdist pool explicitly: SIGTERM to the controller left all 10 workers alive under init. Kill the process group, or sweep by worktree path afterwards and verify zero remain before concluding.

Related: [[feedback_only_reap_own_worktree_processes]] establishes the scoping rule this remedy must respect — reap only processes belonging to your own worktree, never a sibling plan's.

## The timeout verdict was also FALSE — the build had passed

The strongest evidence arrived after this lesson was first written. A fifth attempt returned:

```text
status: timeout
duration_seconds: 1800
timeout_used_seconds: 1800
```

That abandoned process was left to finish rather than killed, and its own build-results log records:

```text
================ 19526 passed, 10 skipped in 1861.97s (0:31:01) ================
```

The daemon reported `timeout` for a build that **passed**, 62 seconds after the budget elapsed. The verdict describes the WAIT, not the WORK — so a caller that reads it as a build outcome records a false negative, re-runs an already-green suite, and (per the section above) stacks another suite while doing so.

⭐ **On a `timeout`, the build's result may still be pending or already decided.** Check whether the process is alive, and read its build-results log once it exits. `timeout` means "this wait reached no verdict" — never "the build failed". Here it did not even mean "no verdict": the verdict was green and unread.

That makes this an instance of the very archetype the plan that found it was fixing — a status reporting one thing confidently while the state it claims to describe says another.
