# Name the rejected flag and the canonical form in argparse-rejection messages

## Context

One plan produced 8 failed script calls across 4 unique signatures, and three of
the four were argparse rejections on three different scripts:

- `plan-marshall:manage-status:manage-status read` — exit 2, twice, at
  2026-09-03T18:22:40Z and 2026-09-03T19:09:44Z, from two *different* dispatched
  envelopes (`automatic-review` and `finalize-step-review-retrospective`).
  Executor message: ``Use a declared flag for `plan-marshall:manage-status:manage-status read`: ['plan-id', 'store']``
- `plan-marshall:manage-references:manage-references get` — exit 2, twice, at
  2026-09-03T16:03:55Z
- `plan-marshall:tools-integration-ci:ci pr prepare-body` — exit 2, twice

## Root cause

The executor's rejection message lists the flags the verb *does* declare, but
never names the flag it rejected and never points at the verb that accepts it.
A caller that reached for `--field` on `manage-status read` is told which flags
exist and is left to infer that `metadata --get --field` is the verb it wanted.
Every rejection therefore costs a diagnostic round trip, and the identical
mistake recurred across two independent envelopes in the same run — which is
what a message that does not teach the fix produces.

## Proposed action

Extend the executor's `failure_kind=argparse_rejection` message with two fields
it already has in hand:

1. the offending flag as the caller wrote it, and
2. when the flag is declared on a *sibling* verb of the same script, that verb's
   canonical form.

The `ci` router already does the second thing for its own position mirror-case
and it demonstrably works; generalising it to the executor's shared rejection
path covers every `manage-*` surface at once.

## Evidence

- aspect: script_failure_analysis — 8 total failures, 4 unique, 3 of 4 argparse rejections across 3 distinct scripts
- aspect: log_analysis — `errors_work: 8`, `errors_script: 8`
- The `manage-status read` rejection fired in two different dispatched envelopes, so this is not one caller's slip

## Additional instances merged from the same run

Message `always-on-is-not-a-resolve-008.md` was filed separately and is folded here rather
than as its own lesson, because its remedy is this one. During the
`project:finalize-step-lessons-housekeeping` re-fire, two consecutive
`manage-references` calls were rejected back to back for *absent required flags*:

```text
[16:03:55] Add the required flag(s) to `...:manage-references get`: ['field']
[16:04:10] Add the required flag(s) to `...:manage-references compute-footprint`: ['worktree-path']
```

Both fired inside one dispatched envelope, and the first rejection did not stop the caller
issuing a second call with the same defect class — which is the behaviour a message that does
not teach its own fix produces. This widens the lesson's population from "the message does not
name the offending flag" to "the message does not arrest the defect class either": a caller
that cannot learn the rule from rejection N repeats it at rejection N+1 within seconds.

Full run tally: **nine argparse rejections across four unique signatures** —
`manage-references get` / `compute-footprint`, `ci pr prepare-body` / `ci pr edit`,
`manage-status read`, and `phase_handshake`. Two of them recurred from *different* dispatched
envelopes 47 minutes apart, so the cause is the invocation surface rather than any one caller.
The `ci` position trap is documented by name in `agent-behavior-rules.md` as recurrence
signatures 2 and 4, and was hit anyway — including by the main orchestrator. Documentation
alone has now demonstrably failed to hold this class; the remedy has to live in the rejection.

## Recurrence — 2026-09-04, plan `domain-glob-seeding` (PR #1406)

Second run-level observation, at a **higher** rate than the first, which is the point: 12
rejections across 7 unique signatures, on 7 distinct scripts, in one plan.

| script | verb | exit | occurrences |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| `manage-solution-outline` | `read` | 2 | **5** |
| `manage-logging` | `decision` | 2 | 2 |
| `manage-status` | `show` | 2 | 1 |
| `manage-findings` | `qgate` | 2 | 1 |
| `manage-change-ledger` | `read` | 2 | 1 |
| `phase_handshake` | `verify` | 1 | 1 |
| `pyproject_build` | `run` | 1 | 1 |

**The five-in-a-row on one verb is the sharpest evidence yet for the "the message does not
arrest the defect class" widening added above.** A caller reached for `manage-solution-outline
read`, was rejected, and reached for it four more times before arriving at the verb that exists
(`list-deliverables`). Five rejections is not a slip; it is a caller with no way to learn the
correct form from what it was told.

The corpus now holds three per-script lessons in exactly this family —
`2026-09-03-11-004` (`manage-status read` declares only `--plan-id`/`--store`),
`2026-09-03-11-005` (`manage-plan-documents` has no `read` verb), and this run's
`manage-solution-outline read` would be a fourth. Filing a fourth per-script lesson would be
corpus growth in place of a fix: the scripts differ, the failure does not. That is why this
observation is folded here rather than raised separately, and it is the argument for putting
the remedy in the shared rejection path where it covers every `manage-*` surface at once.

Two of the twelve (`phase_handshake verify`, `pyproject_build run`) are exit-1 internal errors
rather than argparse rejections and are out of this lesson's scope; the argparse population is
10 across 5 signatures.
