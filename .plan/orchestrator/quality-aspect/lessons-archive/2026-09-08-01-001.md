# Drift recovery re-dispatches refine, which cannot clear a git-ancestry gate

## Context

Plan `sweep-preambles-shipped-accessors` hit `baseline_drift` at the phase-5-execute
entry gate twice, ~23 minutes apart. Both aborts are recorded in
`work/metrics-dispatch-boundaries-5-execute.toon`:

```text
2026-09-07T21:09:49Z  baseline_drift  148442 tokens   20 tool uses  108844 ms
2026-09-07T21:33:11Z  baseline_drift  148063 tokens   25 tool uses  172341 ms
```

The first abort routed to the documented recovery: re-dispatch `2-refine`. Refine ran,
reconciled all 8 findings the baseline check had filed (2 real content conflicts against
upstream #1443 and #1427, 4 suppressed duplicates, 2 false positives — the duplicates and
false positives were themselves an artifact of the locale bug in lesson `2026-09-07-21-001`),
and returned clean with `4 checks passed, 0 flagged`.

Phase-5 was then re-dispatched and **aborted with the identical `baseline_drift`**. The leaf
refused correctly and stated that a third refine would loop.

## Root cause

The two halves of the recovery are testing different things.

- `2-refine` resolves the drift **as content**: it classifies each reconciliation finding,
  decides whether any deliverable needs re-authoring, and marks the findings resolved.
  On this run it explicitly concluded "no re-authoring of this plan's deliverables
  required" and deferred the rebase to phase-6-finalize.
- The phase-5 Step 3 entry gate tests **git ancestry** — effectively
  `git merge-base --is-ancestor origin/main HEAD`.

Refine never advances the branch and never moves `main_sha`, so it cannot change the
predicate the gate evaluates. The gate's input is untouched by the recovery assigned to
it, which makes the second abort structurally guaranteed rather than unlucky. Every further
refine reproduces it at the same cost.

## Observed impact

- **296,505 tokens and ~4.7 minutes of dispatch time** across the two aborted phase-5
  envelopes, which performed no task work. That is 5.7 % of the plan's 5.23 M-token total.
- A full `2-refine` re-cycle (21:10–21:29) on top of it, plus the hand-diagnosis of the six
  spurious findings the locale bug had manufactured.
- The spend is invisible to the retrospective's waste accounting: `error_total_tokens` counts
  only fatal `error` terminations and `retryable_total_tokens` counts only
  `blocked_session_restart` + `harness_cancellation`, so a `baseline_drift` row lands in
  neither bucket and both read `0`.

## How the run actually recovered

The orchestrator diverged from the documented recovery, deliberately and with its rationale
logged (decision `c467fd`), citing persona Principle 8 — establish provenance, never
blind-retry. It took the merge itself: `git merge origin/main` in the worktree, hand-resolved
the 2 genuinely conflicting test files, verified green (25,231 tests), and confirmed
`git merge-base --is-ancestor origin/main HEAD` now passes before re-dispatching.

That worked, and it was the right call. But it was an improvisation the documented workflow
does not sanction, taken under a standing unattended authorization, on the third attempt.

## The downstream cost of the hand-merge

The merge left commit `93b22eca6` on the feature branch. At finalize,
`finalize-step-sync-baseline` then tried `worktree-rebase-to`, which replays the pre-merge
commits and re-hit the exact conflicts the merge had already resolved. The step had to
deviate a second time (decision `a489f7`): abort the rebase and merge `origin/main` instead.

The classifier had reported `no_overlap` while the real rebase conflicted — the documented
best-effort-probe divergence — but the *reason* it diverged here is the merge commit the
earlier drift recovery created. The two steps interact and neither documents the interaction.

## Proposed action

1. **Make the drift recovery advance the branch.** The recovery for a git-ancestry abort must
   perform the git-level action the gate tests — absorb/merge/rebase `origin/main` into the
   worktree — either instead of, or as a step after, the `2-refine` content pass. Re-dispatching
   refine alone is not a recovery for this gate and should not be documented as one.
   Route the content half and the git half separately: refine when a finding needs
   re-authoring, a git-level sync when the ancestry predicate is false.

2. **Bound the retry on the gate's own input.** The drift counter (2/3 here) counts attempts,
   not progress. Refuse a second recovery of the same class when the gate's input — the
   ancestry predicate — is unchanged since the previous attempt, and escalate instead of
   spending another envelope.

3. **Teach `finalize-step-sync-baseline` about a merge commit on the branch.** When the branch
   carries a merge commit, `worktree-rebase-to` is structurally wrong. Detect that and take the
   merge path directly rather than discovering it through a conflicted rebase that must then be
   aborted.

4. **Count `baseline_drift` spend as waste.** It is a recoverable non-completion that performed
   no work; give it a bucket rather than leaving it outside both.
