# Union every shipped PR merge commit when resolving a split plan footprint

## Context

This plan shipped as two PRs after a mid-finalize split forced by a review bot's 100-file cap: #1436 (76 files, merged as `5c1c8b2`) and #1435 (37 files, merged as `681db944`). The shared footprint resolver returned only the 36-file tail.

Three footprint-consuming aspects then graded the plan against 36 of its 112 files:

- `artifact-consistency` emitted an **error** finding — "Recall 32% below 70% threshold" — and listed 76 correctly-shipped files as missing.
- `outline-vs-shipped` and `routing-decisions` consumed the same short footprint.
- `manage-references reconcile-scope` reported `a_c_state: disagree` with a 76-file symmetric difference against a declaration that was in fact perfectly satisfied.

The plan's real declared-vs-realized coverage is 112 of 112 — 100%, zero scope creep. Every one of the 76 "missing" files is present in `5c1c8b2`.

## Root cause

Two layers, and the upper one is the real defect.

The resolver has no tier that unions the merge commits of a plan that shipped under more than one PR. But it could not have such a tier today, because **only the first `create-pr` writes `facts.pr_number` into `status.metadata.phase_steps`**. This plan's status records `create-pr → #1432` — a PR that was closed unmerged and never shipped anything. The two PRs that actually carried the work were created during the split and were never recorded structurally; they survive only in free prose inside `branch-cleanup`'s `display_detail` and in the decision log.

## Proposed action

1. Record every PR a plan ships under as a **list** in `status.metadata`, appended by whatever creates the PR — including the split path — rather than a single `facts.pr_number` written once by the first `create-pr`.
2. Add a footprint-resolver tier that unions the merge commits of that list.
3. Until (1) lands, a resolver that returns a footprint materially smaller than the declared surface should report the shortfall as a coverage caveat rather than letting three consumers grade against it as if it were complete.

## Evidence

- aspect: artifact_consistency — `affected_files_recall: fail`, `declared: 112`, `found: 36`, `recall_pct: 32.1`, `footprint_resolved: true` (it believed it had resolved).
- aspect: outline_vs_shipped, routing_decisions — both consumed the same 36-file footprint until this retrospective supplied the reconstructed union.
- decision log 2026-09-06T17:49:20Z and 17:50:03Z — `pre_push_quality_gate_inactive — kept pre-push-quality-gate on an unknown build verdict: plan footprint unresolvable`. The same blindness, two phases earlier.
- The union was reconstructed here by hand from `git show --name-only` on the two merge commits, which is exactly the work a resolver tier should do.
