# A staged spec premise expires - re-measure at outline, never inherit it

## What happened

A plan was staged with a measured premise: *"~8 tests fail spuriously because nothing stubs the daemon
routing probe."* By the time it launched, the premise was **already false** — both named modules
patched `factory._route_to_daemon` inline, landed in `aafcd1928` / PR #949, **before the spec was even
written**. Its declared dependency was stale too: it named a plan that had since been closed as
superseded and was unrelated to the surface.

The plan re-measured at outline and proceeded on the **re-measured** premise rather than the staged
one. Final census: 661 call sites examined, **0 currently affected**.

Two other plans in the same batch hit the same class:

- One spec's line numbers were stale by construction — its target file had changed twice since the
  spec was written. It was re-grounded by symbol and three of its claims were **refuted or corrected**
  at outline: a hypothesised collision did not exist, and a claimed off-by-one was not where the spec
  said.
- A cross-epic collision recorded at emit time was **discharged within two hours** by the colliding
  PR merging.

## Why it matters

A staged spec is a **measurement with a timestamp**, and nothing on it says so. It reads as a
statement of fact, so a launching plan inherits it as ground truth and builds on it. The staler the
queue, the more confidently wrong the head of it becomes.

The failure is asymmetric and favours the bad outcome: a spec whose premise has been *fixed* leads to
a plan that finds nothing to do and either lands a no-op or invents scope to justify itself. A spec
whose premise has *moved* leads to a plan that patches the wrong location. Neither produces an error —
both produce a confident, well-formed, useless diff.

⭐ The deepest version of this: a plan can be **staged against a defect that a later plan already
fixed**, and nothing in the queue notices. Ownership is recorded at staging time and never re-checked.

## How to apply

- **Re-measure every load-bearing premise at outline. Do not inherit it.** The spec states what was
  believed when it was written; the outline states what is true now. When they disagree, the outline
  wins and the divergence is recorded, not silently reconciled.
- **Verify by SYMBOL, never by line number.** Line numbers in a spec are stale the moment the file is
  touched. A symbol survives edits; a line number survives nothing.
- **Re-verify a cross-epic or cross-plan collision at outline**, not from the note written at emit
  time. One such constraint here was live for under two hours.
- **Re-check the citations too, not just the premise.** A spec that names an owning plan, a dependency,
  or an adjacent surface is asserting those still exist. IDs go stale exactly like line numbers, and a
  citation naming a retired plan is indistinguishable at read time from one naming a live plan.
- **Report the re-measurement as a result.** "Premise confirmed at HEAD" and "premise refuted, re-scoped"
  are both valuable outputs. Silently proceeding on a corrected premise hides that the queue is
  drifting, and queue drift is the thing you actually want to detect.
- ⛔ Do not treat a re-measured zero as failure. A census returning "0 currently affected" is a real
  finding — but state whether the zero is **structural** (cannot occur) or a **discipline property**
  (does not occur today). Those have opposite implications for whether the guard is still needed.
