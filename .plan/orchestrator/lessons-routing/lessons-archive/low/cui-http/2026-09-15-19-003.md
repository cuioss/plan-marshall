# Undeclared-flag argparse rejections recur across manage-* notations when a workflow body describes a call in prose instead of quoting the canonical invocation

## Observation

One plan run produced three distinct `exit_code=2` / `failure_kind=argparse_rejection` events
across three different `manage-*` notations, all flag-level (the caller knew the right verb and
guessed the flag set), and all firing in workflow bodies that describe the call in prose rather
than quoting a canonical invocation:

1. **`plan-marshall:manage-findings` `qgate resolve`**, twice in six seconds (2026-09-15T14:16:41Z
   and 14:16:47Z): first `--status` used where `--resolution` was required, then the corrected
   retry still omitted the mandatory `--phase`. Both are the canonical recurrence signature #5
   already documented in `persona-plan-marshall-agent/standards/agent-behavior-rules.md` — the
   signature is written down and still recurred, evidence about the *placement* of the guidance
   (a foundational standard) rather than its content: it is not reaching the call site inside the
   q-gate-validation / outline-fix workflow bodies.
2. **`plan-marshall:manage-solution-outline`**, in two separate phases on two different verbs:
   `get-deliverable` (phase 4-plan, 14:20:43Z) and `read` (phase 5-execute, 15:05:04Z). The two
   verbs have overlapping but unequal flag sets — `read` accepts `raw` and `section`,
   `get-deliverable` does not — exactly the shape that invites carrying a flag from one verb to
   its near-sibling.
3. **`plan-marshall:manage-references` `get-context`**, once (15:37:10Z, inside `default:create-pr`)
   — the minimal-flag case: the verb declares exactly one flag, `--plan-id`, and the call passed
   something else. Recovered within ~80 seconds; PR #237 was created shortly after.

## Why this is lesson-bearing

Three notations, three phases, at least two different envelopes/agents, one shared shape: none
reached a script body (all rejected at argparse), all are flag-level rather than verb-level, and
all fired at a call site that describes the invocation in prose instead of quoting or xreffing the
owning skill's canonical-invocation block. The `get-context` instance is the strongest evidence the
driver is authoring habit rather than surface complexity — a one-flag verb is the easiest possible
case to get right, and it was still missed.

This is filed as ONE aggregated lesson rather than three per-notation ones, per the executing
plan's own suggested disposition: the per-notation fixes are trivial (quote the right flag), but
the shared authoring gap — the explicit-call-or-xref contract not being applied at these call
sites — is the thing worth recording once, with the cross-notation evidence kept attached so the
recurrence is visible.

**Companion, not duplicate, of `2026-09-08-15-003`** ("Argparse flag-VALUE shape rejections are a
class the invocation checklist does not cover"): that lesson is about a supplied value's *shape*
being wrong (right flag, malformed value); this one is about the *flag name itself* being wrong or
missing (undeclared flag, or a required flag omitted on retry). Both point at the same underlying
corrective — quote the canonical invocation rather than recalling it — from two different failure
shapes.

Three notations in one run, then three DIFFERENT notations in the very next run: six distinct

Both rejection messages were good (each printed the caller's own invocation with the flag moved),

failing in BOTH directions (`manage-config` too-early in the second plan, `manage-architecture`/
`ci` too-late and too-early respectively here).

The corrective below is unchanged by either recurrence — it is reinforced, not revised.

## Corrective

- **Call-site**: workflow bodies that invoke a `manage-*` verb should quote the exact canonical
  invocation inline, or xref the owning skill's Canonical-invocations block, rather than describing
  the call in prose — the explicit-call-or-xref authoring contract, applied at every call site that
  fired here.
- **Retry discipline**: argparse reports only the first problem it hits. A retry that patches the
  single named flag is not a validated invocation — on rejection, re-read the full declared flag
  set (or run `--help`) before retrying, rather than fixing one flag and re-submitting.
- **Sibling-verb hygiene** (`manage-solution-outline`'s `read`/`get-deliverable`, and
  `manage-status`'s `get`/`read` alias pair): where two verbs or verb-aliases share most of their
  surface but differ in a few flags, consider whether they should converge, since a caller reaching
  for the narrower one with the wider one's flags is asking for something the tool could plausibly
  answer either way.
- **Router-vs-subcommand flag position**: before any call whose flag could plausibly sit at either
  level (`manage-config`, `manage-architecture`, the `ci` router), resolve the position from that
  script's OWN canonical-invocation block — never by analogy to a different script's convention,
  even a very similar-looking one, and never assume a single "correct direction" — this class now
  has confirmed failures in BOTH directions.
- **Surface-declaration check**: "does this script declare `--plan-id` (or any habitually-reached-
  for flag) at all?" is a first-class question, not a default assumption — the three live shapes
  observed so far are router-scoped, verb-scoped, and undeclared.
- **Fail-closed ambiguity** (`manage-change-ledger` case): a fail-closed path reachable by an
  argparse rejection is indistinguishable from the same path reached by a genuine empty result.
  Worth considering whether such paths should log which branch they took.

## Scope

Every workflow body across the `plan-marshall` bundle that invokes a `manage-*` verb or the `ci`
router without quoting or xreffing the canonical invocation. Confirmed cross-plan, cross-notation,
and still growing after three consecutive plans: `manage-findings`, `manage-solution-outline`,
`manage-references`, `manage-config`, `manage-change-ledger`, `manage-status`, `manage-architecture`,
`ci` are all now confirmed instances. Given three-for-three plans in this epic have each hit this
class independently, this has crossed from "worth a lesson" to "worth a mechanism" — see the
Follow-Up recorded in `landings/PLAN-08.md`.
