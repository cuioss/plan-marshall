## G1 — Extend the D1(b) carve-out population to the `create_workflow_cli` declarative CLI shape

- **Kind:** incomplete-sweep
- **Severity:** medium
- **Where:** `doc/plans/truthful-signals/060-invented-plan-scoping-flags-are-an-overgeneralized-convention/report-01.md` § D1(b) — the published denominator and the carve-out table; the un-scanned surface is `marketplace/bundles/plan-marshall/skills/manage-change-ledger/scripts/manage-change-ledger.py:272-419`, `marketplace/bundles/plan-marshall/skills/manage-locks/scripts/build_queue.py:651-694`, `marketplace/bundles/plan-marshall/skills/manage-locks/scripts/merge_lock.py:1582-1698`
- **What is wrong:** The denominator is derived entirely from `.add_parser(` nodes and from three `--plan-id` grep patterns (`add_plan_id_arg(`, `add_argument('--plan-id'`, `add_body_consumer_args(`). Three `manage-*` scripts build their CLI declaratively via `create_workflow_cli(subcommands=[{'name': …, 'args': [{'flags': ['--plan-id'], …}]}])` and so match **none** of those patterns. They are the only three (`git grep -n create_workflow_cli -- 'marketplace/bundles/**/*.py'` returns exactly these three under `manage-*`), and they carry **10 verbs and 7 `--plan-id` declarations**, all re-derived at the landed commit `c586d2cb` and re-confirmed at HEAD:
  - `manage-change-ledger.py` — 4 verbs (`worktree-sha`, `append`, `classify-outcome`, `query`), 1 `--plan-id` (`:313`, on `append`).
  - `build_queue.py` — 2 verbs (`acquire` `:662`, `release` `:675`), 2 `--plan-id` (`:667`, `:680`).
  - `merge_lock.py` — 4 verbs (`acquire` `:1597`, `check` `:1622`, `release` `:1635`, `rate-window` `:1660`), 4 `--plan-id` (`:1602`, `:1627`, `:1640`, `:1670`).

  The consequence is two-sided, and the second side is the one that bites:
  1. **Numerator/denominator.** `manage-locks` appears nowhere in `report-01.md` (`grep -n 'manage-locks' report-01.md` → no match). `manage-change-ledger` is named once, described as having "no `.add_parser(` and no `--plan-id` addition — it uses a different dispatch shape … recorded, not resolved here". That sentence is true of the greps and does flag the script as needing inspection, but a reader carrying it forward will not learn that the script declares `--plan-id` on `append`.
  2. **The carve-out set itself is short by three verbs.** D1(b)'s deliverable is the enumeration of **non**-plan-scoped verbs. `manage-change-ledger`'s `worktree-sha`, `classify-outcome` and `query` are genuinely non-plan-scoped (they declare no `--plan-id`) and belong in the carve-out table; they are absent from it. So the blind spot is not only a denominator hedge — it drops members of the very set the deliverable exists to publish.
- **Why it matters:** `plan.md` § Verification makes the published population load-bearing — "A list of exceptions with no denominator is not a carve-out, it is a sample." A carve-out missing three of its own members, derived from a population that silently excludes one construction shape, is that defect one level up.
- **Fix:** In `report-01.md` § D1(b): (a) re-derive the population with a pattern set covering both construction shapes — `.add_parser(` **and** the `create_workflow_cli(` subcommand literals (`'name':` keys), `--plan-id` via the three existing patterns **and** `'flags': ['--plan-id']`; (b) state beside the numbers which construction shapes the counts cover — keep the existing "upper-bound proxy for leaf verbs" hedge, which is accurate about parser-nodes-vs-leaf-verbs, and add a separate coverage sentence, since the two hedges are about different things; (c) add `manage-locks` (`build_queue`, `merge_lock`) to the plan-scoped enumeration; (d) add `manage-change-ledger`'s `worktree-sha` / `classify-outcome` / `query` to the carve-out table under a "ledger / worktree state" scope row, and correct the `manage-change-ledger` sentence to say it declares `--plan-id` on `append` through a different construction shape; (e) make the `manage-maven-profiles` cell use the `(parsers, plan-id)` form the other cells use — it is `(4, 1)`, not `(4)`.
- **Done when:** `report-01.md` § D1(b) names every `manage-*` script that declares `--plan-id` under either construction shape, its carve-out table contains the three non-plan-scoped `manage-change-ledger` verbs, and the published population states which construction shapes its counts cover.

## G2 — Add a recurrence signature for a router-scoped flag placed AFTER the verb

- **Kind:** stale-statement (report) + omission (the rule itself)
- **Severity:** medium
- **Where:** `marketplace/bundles/plan-marshall/skills/persona-plan-marshall-agent/standards/agent-behavior-rules.md:342` — § "Never invent script subcommands", recurrence signature #2; the claim under test is in `report-01.md` § D1(c), second bullet
- **What is wrong:** `report-01.md` states the prose rule "already names this exact signature", citing signature #2. Line 342 reads exactly as the report quotes it (verified verbatim), but signature #2 names the **mirror** defect and prescribes the **opposite** move: "placing `--plan-id` or `--project-dir` immediately after the notation on `manage-architecture` / `manage-config`, where those flags are declared **on the subcommand** … Example: `manage-architecture --plan-id X resolve …` → canonical `manage-architecture resolve --command quality-gate --audit-plan-id X`." Plan 060's failure is `ci checks status --plan-id X`, where `--plan-id` is a **router** flag consumed by `extract_routing_args` (`ci_base.py:570`) before argparse runs and declared nowhere on the parser tree. Executed against the live parser at HEAD: `_root_router_option_strings(parser)` returns an empty set, `ci checks status --plan-id X` → `error: unrecognized arguments: --plan-id X`, exit 2; `ci --plan-id X checks status` parses. So the verb-level placement fails and the pre-verb placement works — precisely the inverse of what signature #2 teaches. All four signatures were read: #1 is verb-paraphrase, #3 is the doubled bundle-prefix (the only ci-specific one), #4 is `--phase` / `--resolution`. None covers a router flag placed after the verb.
- **Why it matters:** The report uses this claim to conclude the prose lane is saturated and that only a runtime error remains genuinely uncovered. A future corpus-enabled run reading that conclusion skips a signature that is in fact absent — and signature #2's worked example, read by a `ci` caller, points *toward* the failing invocation rather than away from it.
- **Fix:** (a) Add a fifth recurrence signature to `agent-behavior-rules.md` § "Never invent script subcommands": **router-scoped flag placed after the verb** — `--plan-id` / `--project-dir` on the `tools-integration-ci:ci` surface are consumed by the router *before* the subcommand token and are declared on no subparser, so they belong ahead of the verb. Worked example: `tools-integration-ci:ci checks status --plan-id X` → canonical `tools-integration-ci:ci --plan-id X checks status`. State explicitly that this is the mirror of signature #2 so the two are not read as one. (b) Correct the `report-01.md` D1(c) bullet to say the rule names the *mirror* signature, not this one. (c) Do **not** add a plugin-doctor positive control for this signature beside the existing four (`test/pm-plugin-development/plugin-doctor/test_analyze.py:1721`, `:1745`, `:1769`, `:1798`) without first fixing G6 — the cluster's flag rule is position-blind by construction (`_analyze_argument_naming.py:283-286` unions each subcommand's flag set with the root parser's), so there is currently no rule for such a control to pin. Pin it once G6 lands.
- **Done when:** `agent-behavior-rules.md` § "Never invent script subcommands" contains a signature whose worked example is a router flag moved from after the verb to before it, and `report-01.md` § D1(c) no longer claims signature #2 names this shape.
- **Module/topic:** `plan-marshall/skills/persona-plan-marshall-agent` (agent-behavior-rules)

## G5 — The shipped router-flag note names a script path no caller may invoke

- **Kind:** wrong-behaviour (error text whose value is what a reader does)
- **Severity:** medium
- **Where:** `marketplace/bundles/plan-marshall/skills/tools-input-validation/scripts/input_validation.py:1010-1016` — the `example` string in `_augment_misplaced_router_flag`; `prog` is supplied at `:1048` as `parser.prog`
- **What is wrong:** The note's worked example is built as `f'{prog} {misplaced[0]} VALUE <subcommand> ...'`. No marketplace script sets `prog=`, so `parser.prog` defaults to `os.path.basename(sys.argv[0])` — the raw script filename. Executed against a parser replicating `architecture.py`'s real root (root `--project-dir` + `add_plan_id_arg(parser)` + subparsers, no `prog=`; `architecture.py:31-38`), with `sys.argv[0]` set to the real script path: `PROG = 'architecture.py'` and the emitted note reads ``… e.g. `architecture.py --project-dir VALUE <subcommand> ...` ``. Two independent defects in one string: the invocation form is one CLAUDE.md § "Script Execution Convention" forbids (all marketplace scripts run through `python3 .plan/execute-script.py {bundle}:{skill}:{script}`, never by path), and `<subcommand>` is a placeholder where the caller's own verb was available in the failing argv.
- **Why it matters:** `plan.md` § Verification sets the acceptance bar for exactly this remedy: "the error message is text-whose-value-is-what-a-reader-does — so it gets a **cold read** … The correct answer is the working invocation. If it cannot tell, the message failed however complete it looks." A cold reader given `architecture.py --project-dir VALUE <subcommand> ...` cannot produce the working invocation: they get a forbidden call form and a placeholder verb. G3 proposes adopting this same mechanism for the `ci` surface, so the defect propagates unless fixed first.
- **Fix:** In `_augment_misplaced_router_flag`, build the example from the caller's actual argv rather than from `prog` plus a placeholder: pass the verb tokens that preceded the misplaced flag and render `{invocation} {flag} VALUE {verb…}`. For the invocation prefix, prefer an explicitly-supplied notation over `parser.prog` — add an optional `notation` parameter that `parse_args_with_toon_errors` fills from the executor-provided environment when present (`.plan/execute-script.py` is the only supported entry point) and fall back to `prog` only when it is absent. Extend `test/plan-marshall/tools-input-validation/test_router_flag_placement.py` with an assertion that the emitted example contains the caller's real verb and not the literal `<subcommand>`.
- **Done when:** For argv `['architecture', 'find', '--pattern', 'X', '--project-dir', '.']` the note's example names `find` (not `<subcommand>`) and does not name a bare `*.py` filename, and a test in `test_router_flag_placement.py` asserts both.
- **Module/topic:** `plan-marshall/skills/tools-input-validation`

## G6 — The `ARGUMENT_NAMING_*` doctor cluster is blind to a documented misplaced router flag

- **Kind:** vacuous-guard (a guard that passes against the defect it is named as guarding)
- **Severity:** high
- **Where:** `marketplace/bundles/pm-plugin-development/skills/plugin-doctor/scripts/_analyze_argument_naming.py:296-303` (`_entry_from_surface`, the `root_flags | child_flags` union) and its justification at `:283-286`; consumed by `scan_flag` at `:525-578`. The claim under test is `report-01.md` § D1(c), first bullet.
- **What is wrong:** `_entry_from_surface` sets each subcommand's accept-set to `root_flags | child_flags`, justified in its docstring as "because argparse honours a root-declared flag (`--plan-id`, `--project-dir`) on every subcommand while rendering it only in the root's help." **That premise is false**, and it is false in exactly the way this plan is about: argparse accepts a root-declared flag only *ahead of* the subcommand. Executed against a parser built like `architecture.py`'s real root, `['architecture','find','--pattern','X','--project-dir','.']` → `error: unrecognized arguments: --project-dir .`, exit 2. Because the union can only ever *add* flags to `allowed`, a router flag declared on the root is unconditionally in the accept-set of every subcommand, so `scan_flag` can never report it. Proven by execution using the cluster's own fixture helpers (`_build_fixture_root`, `_write_fake_script(root_flags=['project-dir','plan-id'], subcommands={'find': ['pattern']})`, `_write_skill_md`, `write_dispatching_executor` from `test/pm-plugin-development/plugin-doctor/test_analyze.py`): a SKILL.md documenting `… architecture find --pattern X --project-dir .` yields `analyze_argument_naming(...) == []` — **zero findings of any rule**, while a genuinely unknown `--nope` in the same position is correctly reported.
- **Why it matters:** `agent-behavior-rules.md:346` names this cluster as "the structural guard against this class of failure", and the cluster's own tests pin four argparse-rejection recurrence signatures as positive controls. The signature that `_augment_misplaced_router_flag` (#1213) exists to rescue at *runtime* is invisible to the guard at *edit* time, for every script that declares its router flags on the root parser — `manage-architecture` and `manage-config` among them. This also undercuts `report-01.md` § D1(c)'s rejection of the doctor-check candidate as "duplicate work": for the plan's own signature the cover does not exist, so a future corpus-enabled run reading that bullet would drop a remedy that is in fact open. Note the asymmetry: on `ci`, whose router flags are *not* declared on the parser, the cluster may still report `ci checks status --plan-id X` — so the blindness is scoped to root-declared router flags, which is the majority shape.
- **Fix:** Add a placement-aware rule to the cluster (e.g. `ARGUMENT_NAMING_ROUTER_FLAG_MISPLACED`): when a documented invocation carries a subcommand and a flag that is in `root_flags` but **not** in that subcommand's own subtree flags, report it with a fix naming the pre-verb position. Keep the existing union for `FLAG_UNKNOWN` so no new false positives appear there, and correct the `_entry_from_surface` docstring at `:283-286` — the union's real justification is over-approximation to avoid false findings, not an argparse behaviour that does not exist. Add positive and negative controls beside the existing four (`test_analyze.py:1721`, `:1745`, `:1769`, `:1798`): the misplaced form is reported, the pre-verb form is not.
- **Done when:** `analyze_argument_naming` returns an `ARGUMENT_NAMING_ROUTER_FLAG_MISPLACED` finding for a SKILL.md documenting `… architecture find --pattern X --project-dir .` against a script declaring `--project-dir` on the root only, returns none for the same doc written `… architecture --project-dir . find --pattern X`, and both cases are pinned in `test/pm-plugin-development/plugin-doctor/test_analyze.py`.
- **Module/topic:** `pm-plugin-development/skills/plugin-doctor`

## G7 — The same doctor cluster mis-scopes a router-flag-before-verb invocation and can flag a correct document

- **Kind:** false-positive guard
- **Severity:** medium
- **Where:** `marketplace/bundles/pm-plugin-development/skills/plugin-doctor/scripts/_analyze_argument_naming.py:131-136` (`_INVOCATION_RE`) and `:537-543` (`scan_flag`'s `inv.subcommand is None` branch)
- **What is wrong:** `_INVOCATION_RE` captures as `subcommand` only the first token after the notation, and that token must not start with a hyphen. A **correctly** written router-flag-first invocation therefore parses as `subcommand = None`, and `scan_flag` judges every flag on the line against `entry.root_flags` alone — the verb's own flags are never consulted. Executed with the cluster's fixture helpers against a script declaring root `--project-dir` / `--plan-id` and a `find` subcommand declaring `--pattern`: the doc line `python3 .plan/execute-script.py plan-marshall:manage-architecture:architecture --project-dir . find --pattern X` yields ``ARGUMENT_NAMING_FLAG_UNKNOWN: Flag `--pattern` not declared on `plan-marshall:manage-architecture:architecture <root>` (known: ['plan-id', 'project-dir'])`` — a finding against a document describing an invocation that parses cleanly. This is the exact inverse of G6: the guard is silent on the failing shape and speaks on the working one.
- **Why it matters:** `plan.md` § Verification states the principle directly for D3(c): "a doctor check that fires on correct docs is a regression." The tree contains at least one real invocation of this shape outside `ci` — `marketplace/bundles/plan-marshall/skills/tools-script-executor/SKILL.md` documents `plan-marshall:manage-architecture:architecture --project-dir . find --pattern "*.py"` — plus fifteen `tools-integration-ci:ci --project-dir {worktree_path} …` lines across `automatic-review/SKILL.md`, `phase-6-finalize/standards/branch-cleanup.md` and `phase-6-finalize/workflow/create-pr.md`. **Not confirmed against the real tree:** whether the cluster reports these today depends on the help-derived root-flag set that `script-shared`'s `argparse_surface` produces, and deriving it requires `.plan/execute-script.py`, which this clone does not have. The rule-level behaviour is proven; the live incidence is not.
- **Fix:** In `_extract_invocations`, skip leading `--flag [VALUE]` pairs when locating the subcommand token, so a router-flag-first invocation resolves to the same `(subcommand, rest)` pair as the flag-last form, and keep the router flags in `rest` for the flag scan. Add a control to `test_analyze.py` asserting that the pre-verb form of a documented invocation produces no `ARGUMENT_NAMING_FLAG_UNKNOWN` for a flag that the named subcommand declares. Then re-run the cluster over the real tree to confirm the seventeen call sites listed above are clean.
- **Done when:** For a script declaring root `--project-dir` and a `find` subcommand declaring `--pattern`, a doc writing `… architecture --project-dir . find --pattern X` produces no `ARGUMENT_NAMING_FLAG_UNKNOWN`, and a test in `test_analyze.py` pins it.
- **Module/topic:** `pm-plugin-development/skills/plugin-doctor`
