= Cloud-run quality analyses: findings
:toc: left
:toclevels: 3

Five epics were exported to `doc/plans/{epic}/` and executed as standalone cloud runs outside the
plan-marshall lifecycle. Each was then measured against the local lane. **The analysis is complete**;
this document is the summary of what the five reports found.

* The per-epic reports are linked below.
* The method that produced them is xref:analyzis_rules.adoc[Analysis rules].
* What a cloud run costs is xref:cost-measurement.adoc[Cost measurement].

[IMPORTANT]
====
**The one-sentence finding: the cloud lane is not broken, it is *thin*.**

It executes well — it lands what it starts, it refutes its own briefs honestly, it does not pad, and
it acts on external review. What it gives up is the local lane's in-run verification machinery, and it
then buys that verification back as a **separate, separately-billed, and entirely optional third
call**. Every corpus-level defect in these five reports traces to that one structural choice.

⇒ **The deficit is not in execution. It is in self-knowledge.**
====

[#_the_two_demoted_corpora]
== The two demoted corpora, and why they are kept

`multiplattform` and `test-quality` carry materially weaker comparative data than the other three, and
this summary treats them accordingly. The weakness is specific:

[cols="2,5",options="header"]
|===
| Corpus | What is missing

| `multiplattform`
| **No `verification.md` and no `gaps.md`** — the third billed bucket never ran, so it publishes **no
  gap count**. No local comparison set. No record of model or effort. **Four of the method's eleven
  steps could not be performed**, over n = 3 plans.

| `test-quality`
| **No local comparison of any kind** — the epic ran no local plans and the shared archive has been
  garbage-collected to n = 2. Its own verification pass was never commissioned either, so its primary
  quality instrument is *the ingestion analysis's own output* rather than an arm's-length pass.
|===

⇒ **Neither contributes a figure to any cross-report aggregate in this document**, and neither is
cited in support of a rate. Where a convergence is claimed below, the population is the three full
corpora.

[NOTE]
====
**They are nonetheless retained, and deleting them would have cost more than keeping them.** Each
produced a methodological finding the strong corpora structurally could not — precisely *because* its
weakness forced a measurement the others took for granted:

* ⭐⭐ **`multiplattform` invalidated the discriminator the other three rest on.** A comment thread's
  `resolved` flag read *post-merge* is not resolution *before* merge. On the two PRs where an
  independent finalize-time figure exists, both moved upward afterwards — `0/3 → 3/3` and `3/6 → 6/6`.
  Every resolution rate in this document set is therefore **resolution-eventually**, an upper bound on
  merge-gate behaviour. The three strong reports could not have found this; none of them had a corpus
  where the two instruments disagreed visibly.
* ⭐⭐ **`test-quality` established that the verification pass is optional in practice, not just in
  contract** — ten plans, sixteen runs, zero verification passes, and nothing anywhere recorded the
  absence. It also found that **all four** of its refuted claims were invalidated *after* merge, by
  changes the claiming plan could not have seen, which makes that class a repository-wide gap rather
  than a lane one.

Both findings are about the *method*, and both survive their corpus's weakness because they rest on
the PR record rather than on anything the lane authored about itself.
====

== What replicated across the three full corpora

Four findings reproduced independently, under different briefs and different instruments. These are
the load-bearing results.

=== 1. ⭐⭐ The run report is self-assessed, and roughly one gap in six corrects it

[cols="3,2,3",options="header"]
|===
| Corpus | Gaps correcting the run's own report | Brief

| `truthful-signals` | **16.6%** | repository-wide checklist sweep
| `code-intelligence-substrate` | **16.3%** | audit of landed plans
| `review-apparatus` | **18.1%** | plan-scoped claim audit
|===

Three corpora, three different questions, three rates within 1.8 points of each other. **The lane's
only account of a run is written by the agent that performed it, and nothing checks that account
before the work merges.** This is the most tightly replicated number in the set.

=== 2. Plans ship deliverables the tree does not support

The instruments differ too much to average, so each is stated with its own denominator:

* `truthful-signals` — **20.4%** of deliverable rows fail at least one of four fidelity axes; **58%**
  of plans carry at least one such row.
* `code-intelligence-substrate` — **75.7%** of plans shipped a deliverable the tree does not support
  as claimed.
* `review-apparatus` — only **9 of 56 deliverables (16.1%)** came through with an unqualified verdict,
  and **13 of 13 plans** carry at least one shortfall.

⚠ These are not the same measurement and must not be read as a trend. What is common to all three is
direction and magnitude: **a substantial minority to a large majority of plan claims do not survive
contact with the tree.**

=== 3. Nothing structurally blocks a merge with an open finding

`truthful-signals` measured **57.1%** reviewer resolution against the local lane's **100%**.
`review-apparatus` measured **100% in both lanes** — but nothing *prevented* the other outcome; the
mechanism is unchanged and merely untested there. `code-intelligence-substrate` reached **95.7%**,
statistically indistinguishable from local.

⇒ **The lane acts on external review well when it acts. It has no gate that requires it to.**

⛔ Every figure in this section is subject to the `multiplattform` correction above and is an **upper
bound**.

=== 4. The lane cannot measure its own cost — *now closed*

All five reports name this. It is the one finding in the set that has since been **resolved**: see
xref:cost-measurement.adoc[Cost measurement].

[cols="3,3",options="header"]
|===
| | Result

| Operator estimate (all five reports) | 2–4% of a weekly limit per plan, explicitly unattributed
| **Derived figure** | **≈3.9% per cloud-lane run**, all-in, from a fully-consumed budget window
|===

**The estimate is confirmed by an independent instrument and lands at the top of its range.** The
three-bucket split — authoring / running / verifying — remains unmeasured; the window method sidesteps
it rather than solving it.

⚠ **The cloud-plan campaign ran two weeks; the figure is derived from the one that is attributable.**
The other week mixed cloud-plan work with unrelated work, so its budget cannot be split between lanes
without guesswork. The headline is therefore a **one-of-two-week sample**, selected on measurability
rather than on being typical, and the campaign's total cost is **not** this figure multiplied by two.

== The structural findings

Each of these appeared in one corpus and is unique to it, but each is a property of the contract rather
than of that epic.

=== ⭐⭐ The lane contract forbids a lane run from repairing the lane contract

`review-apparatus`. A gap whose remedy is an edit to `cloud-plan-lane/SKILL.md` can only be *proposed*
by a lane run. Three such gaps sat open for twelve days; **27 commits crossed that file; none applied
the fix; the fix rotted into a regression.**

⇒ **Ingesting the plans into the local lane dissolved the deadlock by changing which rule-set governs
the run.** No amount of effort inside the lane could have. This is the clearest case in the set where
the lane's problem is structural rather than a matter of thinking harder.

=== ⭐⭐ The lane sizes plans past a reviewer's hard limit

`multiplattform` — and independently reproduced in `test-quality`:

* `multiplattform`: **3 of 3** plan PRs exceeded Sourcery's published 150,000-character limit
  (194k / 377k / 326k). One reviewer of three was disqualified **by construction, not by chance**.
* `test-quality`: two PRs forfeited automated review outright on file-count ceilings — **112 and 309
  files** against a limit of 100.

⇒ **A share of this corpus's missing review is lane-caused, not environmental** — which matters,
because "reviewer coverage is environmental and shared across lanes" was the control that *removed*
coverage as an explanation in the reference report.

=== ⭐ Nothing re-checks a landed claim

`test-quality`. All four of its refuted claims were invalidated **after** merge — three by later work
inside the epic, one by an unrelated epic two days later. **No pre-merge gate in either lane would have
caught any of them.**

⇒ This is the one finding in the set that is **not about the cloud lane**. Nothing anywhere re-checks a
landed claim when a rule changes or a sibling lands, and the rules that would surface it run at
`warning`. Four were found in ten plans the first time anyone looked.

=== The verification pass is optional, and its absence is silent

`test-quality`: ten plans, sixteen runs, **zero** verification passes; nothing recorded the absence.
`code-intelligence-substrate`: plan `500` merged with no verification artefact at all.
`multiplattform`: the whole epic reached *"complete enough to ingest"* with bucket 3 never spent.

⇒ **A missing measurement is not a clean result.** Three corpora reached a state indistinguishable
from "verified" without being verified.

== What the cloud lane is genuinely good at

Measured, not inferred. The reports should not be read as a case against the lane.

* **Throughput.** 51 runs across five epics in seven days; `code-intelligence-substrate` alone landed
  37 plans in 10 days at 3.7 PRs/day, sustained, without an orchestrator session held open.
* **It lands what it starts.** 37 of 37 merged, 0 abandoned.
* **Honest halting and honest partials.** Plans that could not measure a population **refused to
  fabricate the measurement** — one split, shipped the derivable half, and left the rest explicitly
  blocked, reaching that from first principles with no contract rule requiring it.
* **Refutation in flight.** Plans refuted their own spec's premise mid-run and re-scoped rather than
  implementing against a stale brief.
* **The adversarial second pass works.** It is the single highest-yield step in the method: it moved
  verdicts in both directions, caught defects in the *fixes being proposed*, and on one plan converted
  a clean first-pass result into two high-severity findings.
* **Re-entry works** (`test-quality`, the only corpus to exercise it): three later runs closed defects
  their predecessor created or missed.
* ⭐ **Mechanically-derived figures survive; asserted ones do not.** In one plan every census figure
  re-derived exactly while five narrative sections had gone stale.

== Recommendations

Consolidated across the five reports, ordered by measured consequence.

. ⭐⭐ **Make post-run verification a precondition, not an operator option.** Three corpora reached an
  apparently-complete state with the pass never run. This is the highest-value change in the set and it
  is not close.
. ⭐⭐ **Route a gap whose remedy is a lane-contract edit to a non-lane owner at the moment it is
  found.** The proposal-only deadlock is otherwise unfixable from inside.
. **Stop treating the run report as evidence**, or derive its factual rows mechanically. ~1 gap in 6
  corrects it, replicated across three corpora.
. **Give the lane a blocking findings gate.** Nothing structurally prevents merging with an open
  finding.
. ⭐ **Add a periodic re-check of landed claims.** The one recommendation that is not about the cloud
  lane — no pre-merge gate in either lane catches a claim invalidated after merge.
. **Bound authored PR size against the published reviewer limits** — character and file-count both.
  A plan that cannot be reviewed by a third of the roster is mis-sized.
. **Measure reviewer resolution at merge time, not post-hoc.** Until a finalize-time figure is
  captured, no resolution rate in this document set is evidence about a merge gate.
. **Add a mandatory final re-derivation** after the review cycle closes, and after any late fix.
. **Record the model and effort setting in the run report.**
. **Budget the ingestion.** Bringing a cloud wave back into a ledger is a real, recurring, currently
  unbudgeted fourth expenditure — named independently by four of the five reports.

== Conventions

* One file per epic: `{epic}.adoc`.
* Every count rides with its population. A bare `0` must state *which* zero it is — *examined N, none
  found* is a result; *could not look* is not.
* Refuted findings are retained with the evidence that refuted them, never deleted.
* Where an analysis corrects a figure it inherited, the correction is recorded rather than silently
  applied. The `multiplattform` correction above is the worked example.
* These reports carry their measurement instant — a deliberate, stated exception to the project's
  no-timestamps standard, because a comparative measurement without its instant is exactly the
  untruthful signal they exist to find.
