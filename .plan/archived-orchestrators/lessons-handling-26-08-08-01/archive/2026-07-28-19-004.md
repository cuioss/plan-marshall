# The planning-lane router's pre-override input is overwritten by its output, so an operator lane escalation leaves no auditable record of what the router got wrong

## Context

PLAN-10 (PR #1036, epic `test-suite-quality`). The planning-lane router selected the **light**
lane. The operator overrode to **deep**. The deep outline then refuted **three** claims the
plan spec asserted as observed fact:

1. `SECTION_SPEC` had 15 rows, not the 16 the spec stated.
2. The phase-5 marker text **already matched** the consumer regex — the spec had misdiagnosed
   the defect. The real cause was a vacuous guard (`phases[5-execute].status == "pending"`,
   already falsified by the preceding transition).
3. The D3 hypothesis was true but **four times wider** than stated (four structural bypasses,
   not one).

The light lane, by construction, would have discovered none of them: it is the
bounded-localized-change lane and takes the spec's own account of the surface as given. **The
plan would have shipped a fix for a defect it had misdiagnosed, with every gate green.**

## What the artifacts confirm — and what they do not

Confirmed in `status.json` metadata: `planning_lane: deep`, `lane_escalated: true`,
`escalation_trigger: cross_cutting`. The final `references.json` carries
`scope_estimate: single_module`, `track: complex`, and lists 10 affected paths across four
skills plus four test files — plainly not a surgical footprint.

**NOT confirmed by any artifact: the pre-override routing input.** `references.json` holds the
post-escalation values, and `request.md` has no `scope_estimate` section at all. The router's
input at decision time is **overwritten by its output**.

`escalation_trigger: cross_cutting` records *that* an override happened. It does not record
*what the router got wrong*. So there is no way to ask, across plans, "how often does the
router pick light and get overridden, and on what input?" — a systematic misroute cannot be
detected.

## Corrective rule

**Persist the routing decision's INPUT, not just its output.** Record the pre-override
`scope_estimate` and lane recommendation alongside `lane_escalated` and `escalation_trigger`,
so a misroute is auditable after the fact.

This is the load-bearing half and it is independently verified. A second, weaker half is worth
recording as the motivating case but **rests on session narrative rather than a persisted
artifact** — precisely because the input is not persisted:

> A path token appearing in a spec is not evidence that the path is in scope. Specs cite paths
> for reasons that are not "this file will be edited": boilerplate headers, "the shape to copy"
> references (PLAN-10's spec cited `test/_shared/_dispatch_roster.py` twice purely as a pattern
> to imitate), prior-art PR references, and confirm/refute-artifact citations. A scope
> estimator that counts path mentions treats all of these as targets. Derive scope from
> **deliverable-declared targets**, not from a mention count over spec prose.

Adopting the persistence half is what would make the second half checkable on the next
occurrence.

## Impact

Any plan whose spec is written *about* the codebase (audit findings, retrospective follow-ups,
lesson-derived plans) rather than *at* a narrow target — i.e. most of this epic's plans. The
failure is silent and asymmetric: over-scoping costs tokens, but under-scoping ships a fix for
a misdiagnosed defect while every gate reports green, **because the gates verify the change
that was made, not the diagnosis behind it**.
