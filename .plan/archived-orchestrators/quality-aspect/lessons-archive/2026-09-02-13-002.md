# The two finalize row ledgers barely pair, so no single-ledger cost sum is defensible

## Context

`manage-metrics reconcile-ledgers` on this plan reports, for `6-finalize`: **8 of 20 execution-log rows paired** with a dispatch-boundary row.

- **9 `row_absent_from_boundary_ledger`** — `record-step` rows with no boundary partner, including `project:finalize-step-lessons-housekeeping` (113,884 tokens), `project:finalize-step-plugin-doctor` (74,477) and `project:finalize-step-review-retrospective` (119,783).
- **3 `row_absent_from_execution_log`** — terminated dispatches carrying **574,858 tokens** (169,497 + 162,088 + 243,273) that no `record-step` row names. That spend is invisible to any `execution_log` sum.
- `5-execute` adds 3 more one-sided rows (`verify:module-tests`, `verify:quality-gate`, `verify:coverage`).

Independently: the boundary ledger holds **11 rows against 13 dispatched-step firings** implied by `status.metadata.phase_steps` (lessons-housekeeping 1 + plugin-doctor 2 + pre-submission-self-review 5 + simplify 2 + create-pr 1 + automatic-review 1 + review-retrospective 1). Only **4 rows are attributable to `pre-submission-self-review`'s 5 firings** — round 3 (findings filed 23:14:19–23:14:35) has no boundary row between the 23:03:41 and 23:28:03 rows. Its spend sits in the phase total and in neither row ledger.

`channel_completeness` reports `ratio: 0.409` and `confidence: low` — the audit already knows the channel is sparse; what it does not say is that the sparseness is two-sided.

## Root cause

The two ledgers are written by independent call sites with no shared transaction and no shared key (a boundary row carries no `step_id`), so a dispatch can land in one and not the other in both directions — which `reconcile-ledgers`' own contract states. What this plan shows is that the failure is not rare: on a normal run only 40% of rows pair, and the shortfall is not explained by the declared `dispatch_boundary_excluded_classes` (none of which is a finalize step).

The consequence is that every per-step cost figure in a retrospective is drawn from whichever ledger the reader happened to open, and none of them is the population.

## Proposed action

1. Give the dispatch-boundary row a `step_id`, so the join stops being a 300-second timestamp window and starts being a key. That alone converts most of the 12 one-sided rows into either a genuine pair or a genuine gap.
2. Make `union_rows` the figure the retrospective renders for a phase's dispatch population, and mark any single-ledger sum as a floor when `paired_rows < union_rows`. `reconcile-ledgers` already publishes `union_rows`; nothing currently reads it.
3. Find why a dispatch terminates without recording a boundary — the missed round-3 write is the concrete instance to trace.

## Recurrence — 2026-09-02 (domain-over-provision)

Second observation on an independent plan, reproducing the pairing rate almost exactly.

`reconcile-ledgers --plan-id domain-over-provision`: `union_rows: 27`, `execution_log_rows: 24`, `boundary_rows: 11`, `findings_count: 20`. For `6-finalize`: **8 of 20 execution-log rows paired** — the identical 40% figure the original observation records, on a plan with a different footprint, a different task count and a different finalize step set.

- **12 `row_absent_from_boundary_ledger`** in `6-finalize`, five of them carrying real spend: `pre-submission-self-review` twice (231,477 and 175,418 tokens), `project:finalize-step-lessons-housekeeping` (121,264), `create-pr` (100,770), `project:finalize-step-plugin-doctor` twice (85,534 and 79,996).
- **1 `row_absent_from_execution_log`** carrying 237,647 tokens that no `record-step` row names.
- `5-execute` adds 4 more one-sided `verify:*` rows plus one 579,821-token dispatch absent from the execution log.
- `boundary_never_closed` on `6-finalize` — 9 boundary rows totalling 1,285,895 tokens with no `end_time` on the phase row.

Two details sharpen the original diagnosis:

1. **`pre-submission-self-review` is again the worst-covered step.** It fired 4 times; only 2 of those firings have a boundary partner, and the two unpaired ones carry 406,895 tokens between them. The original observation traced its missing round-3 write; this plan shows the same step losing two writes out of four, which makes a step-specific cause more likely than a random dropped write.
2. **The 0.409 `channel_completeness` ratio reproduced to three decimal places.** That the two independent plans land on the same ratio suggests the shortfall is structural — a fixed set of call sites that never record — rather than a sporadic missed write. Proposed action 3 should start by partitioning the finalize step roster into "records a boundary" and "does not", instead of tracing individual misses.

Proposed actions 1 and 2 remain unimplemented; nothing yet reads `union_rows`, and this retrospective's own dispatch figures are consequently floors.

## Recurrence — 2026-09-03 (plan-170-make-the-attribution-usable)

**Third observation, and it refines the structural diagnosis in recurrence 2 rather than merely repeating it.**

`reconcile-ledgers --plan-id plan-170-make-the-attribution-usable`: `union_rows: 36`, `execution_log_rows: 23`, `boundary_rows: 23`, `findings_count: 26`. For `6-finalize`: **9 of 18 execution-log rows paired** against 16 boundary rows.

- **9 `row_absent_from_boundary_ledger`** in `6-finalize`, three carrying real spend: `project:finalize-step-lessons-housekeeping` (178,553), `project:finalize-step-plugin-doctor` (116,029), `create-pr` (127,942). The other six are zero-token inline steps.
- **7 `row_absent_from_execution_log`** in `6-finalize` carrying **1,355,544 tokens** that no `record-step` row names.
- `5-execute` adds 4 one-sided `verify:*` rows plus **4 dispatches absent from the execution log carrying 1,006,245 tokens**.
- **Combined: 2,361,789 tokens — 37.9% of the plan's 6,237,987 total — appear in exactly one ledger.** Neither ledger alone sees more than **64%** of dispatches.
- `boundary_never_closed` on `6-finalize` again: 16 rows, 3,031,240 tokens, no `end_time`.

### What is new

1. **The two ledgers were the same size and still disagreed on a third of the population.** `execution_log_rows` and `boundary_rows` are both 23 here, which is exactly the shape that makes a single-ledger sum look trustworthy — equal counts invite the assumption that they cover the same events. They do not: `union_rows` is 36. A reader checking only the two totals would have found them consistent and been wrong by 13 rows.
2. **`pre-submission-self-review` is the worst-covered step for the third consecutive plan**, and this time three surfaces disagree on its firing count: the boundary ledger records **5** dispatches, `status.metadata.phase_steps` records `firing_count: 3`, and only **2** `record-step` rows name it. Recurrence 2 proposed partitioning the step roster into records/does-not-record; this plan shows the step is not consistently in either partition — it recorded some firings on each surface and different ones. That points at the re-fire path specifically, not at the step's call site in general.
3. **`channel_completeness.ratio` moved** — 0.346 here against the 0.409 that reproduced twice. So the ratio is not the fixed constant recurrence 2 inferred from two samples; it varies with the inline/dispatched step mix. The structural conclusion (a fixed set of non-recording call sites) still holds for the *inline* steps, but the ratio itself should not be treated as a signature.

Proposed actions 1, 2 and 3 all remain unimplemented across three observations. `lessons-housekeeping` retained this entry on both firings during this run, recording it as "2nd independent observation, still unapplied".
