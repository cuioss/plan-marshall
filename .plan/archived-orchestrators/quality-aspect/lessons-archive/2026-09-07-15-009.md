# When a review finding offers two remedies, a prose-only sweep must take the narrowing one and name its own limit

**Signal**: `pr-comment` finding `5b86f8` (CodeRabbit, PR #1436, `test/plan-marshall/test_triage_loop_back_target.py:149`), resolution `fixed` in `be27eb720`.

## Observation

The finding offered an explicit either/or: *"Add assertions for the actual forbidden dispatch and bookkeeping forms, **or** narrow the docstring to the checks shown."*

One remedy adds test coverage. The other corrects a claim. They are not equivalent work, and only one of them was in this plan's scope — a prose-only sweep. The run took the narrowing remedy and recorded the strengthening remedy as a follow-up.

## The transferable rule

A two-remedy finding is a **scope decision disguised as a fix choice**. Picking the wrong branch is how a bounded sweep silently becomes a coverage-expansion plan: adding the assertions would have been real added behaviour, unreviewed against the plan's declared deliverables and outside the footprint the plan declared.

The selection rule that generalises:

- Take the remedy that **restores agreement between the claim and the existing code**. That is always in scope for a plan that touches the claim, because the plan is what put the claim under review.
- Defer the remedy that **changes what the code does or verifies**, and record it as a follow-up rather than dropping it.

## The second half: a narrowed claim must state its own limit

Narrowing is only honest if the narrowed text says what it no longer covers. The fixed docstring names the three observables it actually asserts (`--loop-back-target` absent, `FIND-only` present, `finalize-feedback` mentioned) **and** names its own gap: a dispatch reintroduced without the flag would still pass.

Without that last sentence, narrowing degrades into hiding — the claim becomes technically true and the reader is left believing the test guards a contract it does not guard. A narrowed docstring that does not name its residual gap has moved the defect from the prose into the reader's head.
