# Stamp phase boundaries on loop-back so execute does not absorb finalize time

## Context

The metrics of plan `plan-lb-29-harness-sync` report 5-execute at 21h29m wall clock with 18h26m idle, and 6-finalize with a start of 2026-10-09T12:28:11Z and no end. The dispatch record tells a different story: the first finalize dispatch ended at 2026-10-08T19:21:32Z, and 19 finalize dispatches ran from then on. The 17 hours between the first end of execute and the last re-entry into finalize were spent in finalize: six in-house review rounds, an overnight pause, PR review, and two 90-minute waits on a review bot's quota. All of it is booked on the execute row.

## Root cause

A finalize loop-back sets the phase back to 5-execute and later returns to 6-finalize, but the only metrics call on that path is the close of 5-execute. That close measures its span from the previous close of the same phase, so everything since then, including the finalize work in between, is added to execute. 6-finalize is never closed on the way out and its start is overwritten on the way back in, so its row keeps only the last re-entry. Whether the missing calls belong in the loop-back workflow or in the close logic was not established here.

## Proposed action

- On every loop-back out of finalize, call `phase-boundary --prev-phase 6-finalize --next-phase 5-execute`, and the reverse on the way back, so each phase's span covers only the time it was the current phase.
- Alternatively make the re-close of a phase start its new span at the moment the phase was re-entered (the status record already knows that instant) rather than at its previous close.
- Add a test: execute closes, finalize runs for an hour, loop-back, execute runs for ten minutes and closes; the execute row must grow by ten minutes, not seventy.
