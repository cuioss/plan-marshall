# A staged spec's checked_at is a staleness clock, and a spec must never reserve a monotonic resource by assumption

## What was stale

`PLAN-03-outbound-hostname-verification-core.md` was staged against `checked_at d922fb0`. By the time it launched:

1. **Load-bearing:** deliverable 3 and its `expected_surface` cited `doc/adr/0003-*.adoc` as the next free ADR ordinal (after `0002-*`). Actual: `doc/adr/` held `0001`-`0007`; ordinals `0003`-`0007` had landed via unrelated plans **from this same epic**. Next free ordinal was `0008`.
2. **Non-load-bearing:** the spec cited `cui-java-parent 1.5.9`; HEAD was on `1.5.10` and moved to `1.5.11` mid-flight. The mechanism and file/line claims survived.

Both were caught in `2-refine` and cost only the correction. ⚠ They were caught by a human-grade re-read, **not by a mechanism**.

## Why this is an epic-level rule, not a plan-level one

The staleness was **self-inflicted by the epic's own throughput**: sibling plans in the same epic consumed the ADR ordinals that the spec had reserved by assumption. Any epic that stages several specs up front against one HEAD and then lands them serially generates exactly this drift — and **the later a spec launches, the staler it is**.

## The rules

- A staged spec's `checked_at` is a **staleness clock**, not a provenance note. Any spec whose `checked_at` predates a sibling landing must be re-grounded before it is emitted as a `/plan-marshall` command.
- ⛔ **Never let a spec reserve a monotonic resource by assumption** — ADR ordinals, migration numbers, port allocations, step-order slots. **State the selection rule** ("the next free ADR ordinal"), never the value (`0003`).
- The re-grounding verdict field (`corpus set-verdict` / `corpus verdicts`) is the mechanism that exists for the first rule. Catching it at `2-refine` works but is downstream of where the epic could have caught it.
