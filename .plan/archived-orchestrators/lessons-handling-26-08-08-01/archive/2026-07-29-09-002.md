# Record the signals that refused to lie — the counterexample set is evidence too

## What happened

PLAN-89 / PR #1044 filed a retrospective message titled *"Four signals refused to lie this run — the
counterexample set."* Across a programme whose entire subject is **confident signals that hide a
caveat**, every other message in the corpus records a signal that misled. This one recorded the
signals that held.

That asymmetry matters. A corpus containing only failures supports one conclusion — *the signals are
unreliable* — and cannot distinguish it from the true one: **some signal designs hold under pressure
and some do not.** Without counterexamples there is nothing to generalize *from*, only things to
avoid.

## Solution

**Rule:** when a retrospective enumerates defective signals, it should also enumerate the
**signals that were load-bearing and correct** in the same run — with the same specificity.

- Name the signal, what it asserted, and **what would have made it lie**. A counterexample is only
  useful if the near-miss is stated: *"this could have reported X and did not, because Y."*
- Prefer counterexamples from the **same surface** as a recorded failure. Two designs on one surface,
  one holding and one not, is the strongest available evidence about **which property** is doing the
  work.
- ⚠ **Do not pad.** A signal that was never under pressure is not a counterexample; listing it dilutes
  the set and reintroduces volume-read-as-coverage at the reporting layer.

## Impact

Turns a defect corpus into a **design corpus**. The recurring archetypes recorded so far — vacuous
guards, volume-read-as-coverage, doc-contract divergence, frozen-vs-live — are all stated as
*failure* patterns. Their positive duals (a population-derived detector whose `unclassified` bucket
stayed empty; a fail-closed barrier that actually blocked; a probe that reported `unknown` instead of
`0`) are what a future author should copy, and they are currently recorded only by accident.

⭐ Concretely, in the same window: PLAN-99's classifier reported **`unclassified: 0` in every phase**,
meaning its population-derived design covered the whole population on first contact. That is the
positive dual of the vacuous-guard archetype, and it is worth more to a future detector author than
another instance of the failure.
