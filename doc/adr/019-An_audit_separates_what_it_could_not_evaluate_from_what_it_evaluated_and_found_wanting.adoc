== Status

Proposed

== Context

A large part of this system exists to answer questions of the form _"is there
anything wrong here?"_ — rule analyzers, quality gates, content searches, log
readers, residual checks, derivation tools, retrospective aspects. Each returns a
count, a verdict, or a set. Almost all of them can answer that question in two
structurally different ways that share a single representation:

* **Evaluated, and nothing was wanting.** The surface examined its whole subject
  and found no defect. The zero is a measurement.
* **Could not evaluate.** The subject was out of reach — not configured, not
  resolvable, not parseable, outside the scope actually swept, or subject to a
  predicate that **could not be applied** because the data or grammar it needs was
  unavailable. The zero is the absence of a measurement.

⛔ **The discriminator is whether the predicate could be APPLIED, never whether it
matched.** A predicate applied successfully across the whole subject and matching
nothing is a completed evaluation returning a true zero, and it belongs in the first
category. Reading such a scan as "could not evaluate" is the mirror image of the defect
this ADR exists to prevent, and it is just as damaging: it manufactures doubt about a
sound result, which trains readers to discount the coverage signal entirely.

Rendered identically, these two states are indistinguishable to every consumer,
and they are indistinguishable in the direction that does harm: the second reads
as the first. An unevaluated subject presents as a clean one. The failure is
**silent, fail-open, and self-reinforcing** — a gate that cannot look reports
that it looked and saw nothing, and the reader downstream banks the reassurance.

The shape is not confined to one subsystem. It recurs independently across
surfaces that share no code, which is what makes it architectural rather than a
family of bugs:

* A **scoped rule analyzer** reports clean when its cross-cutting rules anchor
  their findings outside the paths it was given — the rules never ran, and
  "0 findings" covers only the rules that could.
* An **integration gate** reports a verdict for a provider that is not
  configured, so a step that performed no scan renders beside steps that did.
* A **residual check** reports zero residue when it had no baseline to diff
  against — the count is the arithmetic of an empty comparison.
* A **content search** whose pattern is a regular expression returns zero for a
  literal-looking query carrying a metacharacter, and returns it in the same
  shape as a genuine absence.
* A **filtered log read** whose filter names a discriminator the log does not
  carry returns a well-formed empty result set for a population with many
  entries.
* A **metrics roll-up** reports a zero count for an activity the same run
  recorded many times, because the two sides key on different identities.
* A **derivation over declarative sources** collapses "claimed by a source I
  cannot parse" into "claimed by nobody", manufacturing a defect out of the
  parser's own limits.
* A **filesystem probe** treats one negative lookup at one expected location as
  proof of absence, when the subject was merely somewhere else.

Three existing decisions already settle this question inside their own domains —
ADR-009 for version-staleness reporting, ADR-014 for aggregation over
independent producers, ADR-017 for validators resolving a contract they cannot
read. Each is correct and each is scoped. What is missing is the general rule
those three are instances of, so that the next surface with the same choice point
is not left to rediscover it. The structural choice is therefore: does a
measurement surface owe its consumer an account of its own coverage, or may it
report a bare result and let the consumer assume the coverage was total?

== Decision

**A surface that measures, audits, or searches reports its coverage alongside its
result. A clean verdict asserts that the subject was evaluated and found sound;
it is never the representation of an evaluation that did not happen.**

Three obligations follow, and they bind together:

**1. Carry a coverage discriminator.** Every such surface emits, beside its
result, the facts a consumer needs to tell a measurement from its absence — what
population was actually examined, what was skipped, what could not be read, what
was truncated or elided. The result alone is never the whole answer.

**2. Reserve the clean verdict.** A negative is trustworthy only when every
coverage condition is clean together. When any of them is not, the disposition is
**coverage gap**, not "absent" — reported as such, naming what could not be
reached. The canonical formulation of this test already exists as the
complete-coverage rule governing inventory-scoped content search; this ADR
generalises it rather than restating it per surface.

**3. Give "could not evaluate" its own state.** A two-valued result has no room
to express absence of evidence, so it degenerates into the reassuring value. The
enum carries a distinct third member — `unmeasured`, `not_available`,
`not_derivable`, `unknown`, `inconclusive`, as the domain's vocabulary requires —
and that member is never silently promoted to the clean one. Where a surface
reports a tally per rule, per producer, or per aspect, the third state is carried
**per element**, because a run that is clean on nine rules and blind on the tenth
is not a clean run.

The rule binds the **producer**, not the consumer. A consumer that must
reconstruct coverage from a bare zero has already lost the information; the
producer is the only party that knows what it could not reach, and the obligation
sits where the knowledge is.

Two boundaries keep the rule decidable. It governs surfaces whose output invites
the inference _"nothing is wrong"_ — it does not turn every function returning a
collection into a coverage-reporting instrument. And it is satisfied by
**reporting**, not by refusing: a surface that cannot evaluate its subject still
returns successfully, still reports everything it did evaluate, and marks the
remainder. Escalating a coverage gap to a hard failure is a separate decision the
consumer owns, and one this ADR does not make for it.

== Consequences

=== Positive

* A zero from a conforming surface **whose coverage is complete** is **evidence**,
  and can be acted on as such. The value of a clean report rises precisely because it
  can no longer be produced by a surface that did not look. A zero reported *alongside
  a coverage gap* is evidence only about the subset actually evaluated, and carries no
  claim about the remainder — which is why obligation 1 makes the gap visible rather
  than leaving the consumer to assume totality.
* Coverage gaps become **findable work**. An unmeasured subject that announces
  itself is a task; one that renders as clean is invisible, and stays invisible
  for exactly as long as nobody independently rediscovers it.
* The three domain-specific decisions gain a common parent, so a new surface
  facing the same choice point inherits a settled answer instead of re-deriving
  one.
* Audit surfaces compose honestly. An aggregate over conforming producers can
  distinguish a genuinely clean subject from one nobody examined, which a
  roll-up over bare counts cannot do at any level.

=== Negative

* Every conforming surface carries a wider output contract, and its consumers
  carry the branch that reads it. This is real cost paid at many sites.
* Reports get louder and less flattering. A conforming system surfaces its own
  blind spots continuously, and the honest verdict is frequently "less was
  checked than it appeared".
* Retrofitting is not mechanical. Deciding what a given surface's coverage
  conditions actually are is domain work, and cannot be applied by a sweep.

=== Risks

* **The discriminator becomes ceremony.** A surface can emit a coverage field
  that is always clean because nothing ever computes it, reproducing the original
  defect one level up with more structure. Mitigation: the coverage fields must
  be derived from the same pass that produces the result, never asserted beside
  it.
* **Third states get promoted at the boundary.** A consumer that maps
  `unmeasured` onto the clean value for convenience — a dashboard, a summary
  line, a roll-up count — reintroduces the conflation the producer avoided. The
  rule's value survives only as far as the last hop that preserves it.
* **Alarm fatigue.** A system honest about its blind spots reports many, and a
  reader who cannot rank them may learn to skip the coverage section wholesale.
  This is a real cost of the decision, accepted on the grounds that an
  unrankable gap is still strictly more information than a false clean.

== Alternatives Considered

**Let the consumer establish coverage.** Each caller checks the preconditions it
cares about before trusting a result. Rejected: the producer holds knowledge the
consumer structurally cannot recover — which rules were out of scope, which paths
were unreadable, which predicate matched nothing — so the check is being asked of
the one party unable to perform it. It also multiplies by the number of callers a
duty that is discharged once at the source.

**Fail hard when coverage is incomplete.** A surface that cannot evaluate its
subject raises rather than reports. Rejected: it collapses two legitimately
different situations — an optional integration that is simply not configured, and
a gate that was supposed to run and could not — into one blocking outcome, and it
converts every partial answer into no answer. Partial coverage honestly labelled
is more useful than an exception, and whether a given gap should block is the
consumer's decision, not the producer's.

**Document the caveat instead.** State in each surface's contract which
conditions make its zero untrustworthy. Rejected: prose does not travel with the
value. The reader who most needs the caveat is reading a result, not a standard,
and the caveat is discoverable only by someone who already suspects the problem.

**Keep solving it per domain.** Continue adding domain-scoped decisions as each
surface encounters the choice point. Rejected on evidence: the shape has recurred
independently across surfaces sharing no code and no authors, which is the
signature of a missing general rule rather than a run of similar bugs. The
per-domain decisions remain correct; what they lack is a parent that makes the
next one unnecessary.

The rejected options share one property, and it is why they fall together: each
leaves the distinction between _"nothing is wrong"_ and _"nothing was checked"_
recoverable only by a party that does not hold the facts needed to recover it.
