# A spec's own assertion about its write surface is not evidence for an emit-time disjointness decision

## Observation

An orchestrator paired two plans as surface-disjoint. `PLAN-45`'s spec **asserted no production or
test source change**; `PLAN-34`'s declared write surface included
`integration-tests/src/test/java/**`. The emit-time disjointness check was made **against PLAN-45's
assertion**.

**The assertion did not hold.** PLAN-45's realized footprint carried a one-line javadoc edit in
`integration-tests/src/test/java/.../BffKeycloakLoginFlow.java` — updating a doc path in a comment.
⇒ **The two plans overlapped on a file the disjointness call had ruled out.**

Consequences were mild here — the edit is auto-mergeable and both running worktrees already carried
the file, so they rebase cleanly. ⛔ **The mildness is luck, not design.** The same mechanism with a
non-trivial edit is a rebase conflict across two concurrent plans, discovered at finalize.

⭐ **Second firing of this watch in one epic**, which is what makes it a rule rather than an incident.

## Why the usual defence does not apply

A spec is **authored before the work**. Its statement about what it will touch is a **prediction by
the author**, and the orchestrator consumes it as though it were a measurement. That is the same
class as a frontmatter declaration standing in for an enforced property — **an assertion by the
author, not a guarantee by the system** — except the consumer here is a *scheduling decision*, so the
failure surfaces as a collision rather than a wrong number.

⛔ And the failure is **silent at decision time**: nothing at emit can distinguish a spec whose
assertion will hold from one whose will not.

## Rule

- **Never let a spec's own prose about its write surface be the sole basis for a disjointness call.**
  Treat it as a **hypothesis about a footprint**, ranked below anything derived.
- **Prefer derived evidence where it exists**: the module/path attribution of the surfaces the spec
  actually names, a sibling plan's *realized* footprint from its merge commit, or an explicit
  path-level check — not the spec's summary sentence.
- **A negative assertion is the weakest form of all.** *"This plan touches no source"* is
  unfalsifiable at emit and is defeated by a single incidental edit — a javadoc line, an import
  reorder, a generated stamp. ⭐ **When a plan genuinely must not touch source, that has to be
  enforced by the deliverable excluding source edits outright**, not requested in prose; a request
  the pipeline cannot check is not a constraint.
- **Record the basis of every disjointness call** (declared vs derived), so a later collision
  identifies which kind of evidence failed rather than re-litigating the pairing.

## Impact

Every emit-time pairing decision in the orchestrator tier, `parallelization_scope > 1` in particular,
and every spec that states a negative write-surface claim. Related: the archetype that a declaration
carrying operational weight needs a producer that observes it or a consumer that rejects a violation.
