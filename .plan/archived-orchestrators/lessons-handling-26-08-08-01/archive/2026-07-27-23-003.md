# Compose-time force-out declared via lane=off does not remove its step — enforcement lives only in a run-time check

Observed during `finalize-dispatch-audit-integrity` (PR #1026), alongside a
sibling compose-time-predicate defect (the pre-push-quality-gate
footprint-emptiness omit) that has since been fixed at the predicate.

## The defect — a force-out that does not remove anything

`decision.log` entry `de524e`:

> Skipped on operator intent: `step_params` carries `lane=off`, which the
> `ext-point-lane-element` contract defines as force-out unconditional. NOTE an
> inconsistency worth a follow-up — the step is nevertheless PRESENT in the
> composed manifest `phase_6.steps`, so the `lane=off` force-out did not remove
> it at compose time.

The dispatcher honoured the operator, which is correct. But `execution.toon` —
the artifact that persists and that every downstream consumer reads as the
authoritative step set — still lists `adr-propose`. Enforcement lives entirely in
a run-time check choosing `step_params` over manifest presence, so anything that
reads `execution.toon` directly — step counts, coverage assertions, a
retrospective's own manifest and routing aspects — is reasoning over a document
that does not describe the run.

## Guidance

- **Apply force-out semantics where the contract says they apply.** `lane=off` is
  defined as unconditional removal; enforce it at compose so the manifest is the
  single source of truth, and keep the run-time check as a backstop rather than as
  the mechanism.
- **A decision log entry that states a reason is not evidence the reason was
  real.** The defect wrote confident, well-formed reasoning into `decision.log`
  while the manifest it described did not match. Audit predicates and transforms
  by asking what their OUTPUT actually contains, not by reading what they logged.
