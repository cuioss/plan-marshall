# A self-review guard's scope, indexing, matched control, and population must each be checked against its load-bearing site, not a synthetic fixture

## Context

PLAN-TRUTH-157's own deliverable 6 (matched controls for write-path containment and effort-pin
independence) hit six distinct, separately-diagnosed ways a self-review/detector guard can be vacuous or
wrong, each caught by CodeRabbit or Q-Gate during the plan's own review rounds:

1. **Positional indexing instead of naming.** `orchestration-model.md` stated "the last four" of a
   seven-item dispatch index are drafting dispatches. Positionally that is entries 4-7 — which includes a
   READ dispatch (entry 6) and excludes a genuine drafting one (entry 3). The real drafting set (3, 4, 5,
   7) had to be corroborated against the actual source documents, not the ordinal claim.
2. **An affirmative-verb allowlist that is incomplete by construction.** `_AFFIRMATIVE_RE` required one of
   seven specific verbs to match before a write-grant could be detected at all — so "records", "authors",
   "sets", "mutates", "is responsible for writing" all passed an absence-claim test green while carrying
   an unambiguous, unmatched grant.
3. **The fix for (2) introduced a fresh vacuity one round later.** Widening the affirmative regex to
   include bare `write` self-defeated: because the scan lower-cases and substring-matches the FORBIDDEN
   target too, and `write` is itself a forbidden target, naming that one target auto-satisfied the
   affirmative condition — the exact inversion the fix's own docstring said it avoided.
4. **An unanchored pattern matched an unrelated, pre-existing phrase.** A bare-phrase pattern with no
   anchor to the specific constraint it verified was satisfied by unrelated prose containing the same
   substring ("selection rules" contains "selection rule"). Verified live: deleting the sentence the
   pattern was SUPPOSED to check left the assertion green.
5. **A matched control transcribed a SUBSET of the live constant it stood in for**, disagreeing with the
   real vocabulary in BOTH directions (false-green when the real token set shrinks, false-red on an
   unrelated rephrase) — the exact vacuity its own assertion message claimed to prevent. The remedy is to
   assert with the constant itself, never a hand-copied excerpt of it.
6. **A non-vacuity guard fired only on TOTAL emptiness, not a shrunk population.** A guard chained through
   two filters (`drafting` filtered from `derived`) only tripped when BOTH source documents dropped out —
   the single-document case, where the population merely shrank, was silent.

Also folded in from the same cluster: a self-review guard checking a NEGATED clause must scope the
negation to the CLAUSE carrying the affirmative verb and its target, not the whole SENTENCE — a grant
sharing a sentence with any negation word is silently dropped at sentence scope (the load-bearing sentence
in `orchestration-model.md` itself is semicolon-joined and mixes a grant with a prohibition, which is
exactly where this bites hardest). See lesson `2026-09-07-21-001` and `2026-09-11-15-001` for the more
general "guard tested on only one direction" principle this cluster specializes.

## Root cause

Every instance shares one shape: a guard author chose a convenience (an ordinal position, a short verb
list, a substring match, an unanchored phrase, a hand-copied constant excerpt, a total-emptiness check) in
place of the thing the guard actually needed to assert (a named member, a semantically complete
vocabulary, a clause boundary, an anchored statement, the real constant, a per-member check). The
convenience passes every synthetic fixture and fails exactly at the corpus's own load-bearing site.

## Proposed action

For every self-review/detector guard: name members explicitly rather than indexing positionally; derive
an affirmative/negative vocabulary from the real constant the guard is meant to enforce, never
transcribe a subset; scope negation and other suppression patterns to the clause carrying the target, not
the sentence; anchor a presence pattern to the specific statement it verifies, not a bare phrase that can
match unrelated prose; and make a non-vacuity check fire per-member on a shrinking population, not only on
total emptiness. Verify every one of these against the corpus's own most load-bearing instance, not a
synthetic fixture built for the test.
