# Directory and glob claims are dropped by both the queue renderer and the disjointness check, so absence reads as disjointness

# The Ordered Queue's surface column silently drops directory entries, understating every surface a reader pairs on

## Context

`orchestrator resume-summary` emits an `ordered_queue` table whose `Surface (expected)`
column is derived from each staged spec's `## Expected Surface`. That column is what a
reader consults to judge whether two plans are disjoint enough to run concurrently — it is
the queue table's whole reason for carrying a surface column at all.

Across a 22-row corpus, **not one directory-shaped entry appears anywhere in the rendered
table**. Only file-shaped entries survive. Two rows show the effect at full strength:

- **PLAN-155** renders `(no expected surface)`. Its spec carries **14** entries, every one a
  directory (`test/plan-marshall/extension-api/`, `.../script-shared/`, and twelve more).
- **PLAN-110** renders three files (`test/README.md`, `test/conftest.py`,
  `test/test_conftest_discipline.py`). Its spec carries **15** entries, twelve of them
  directories — including `test/pm-plugin-development/`, `test/marketplace/` and
  `test/plan-marshall/phase-6-finalize/`, which are whole subtrees.

A second, independent derivation over the same sections disagrees: the epic-surface
partition tool's `classify` resolves 15 entries for PLAN-110 and 14 for PLAN-155. Two
derivations reading one section, returning 3-of-15 and 0-of-14 against 15 and 14.

The direction of the error is the dangerous one. A surface that renders as three files
looks disjoint from almost everything; the same surface honestly rendered claims three
subtrees and collides with most of the queue. `(no expected surface)` reads as *"this plan
touches nothing"* — the strongest possible disjointness signal — for a plan that claims
fourteen directories.

## Root cause

Not established from the outside. The observable is that the renderer's entry filter admits
file-shaped paths and drops directory-shaped ones, rather than rendering a directory as the
recursive claim it is. `(no expected surface)` is emitted when the filter admits nothing,
so a spec whose surface is entirely directories is indistinguishable from a spec with no
surface section at all — and those are different facts.

## Proposed action

Render directory entries as directories, with their trailing separator preserved, so a
recursive claim is visible as one. Where the column must stay short, truncate with a stated
count (`+N more`) rather than by silently dropping a shape class.

Distinguish the two zeros: a spec with **no** `## Expected Surface` section and a spec whose
surface **resolved to nothing renderable** are different states and must not share the
`(no expected surface)` rendering. The second should say that entries exist and were not
rendered.

Consider deriving the column from the same resolver the partition tool's `classify` uses, so
two derivations over one section cannot disagree.

## Evidence

- `orchestrator resume-summary --slug {epic}` `ordered_queue`: 11 staged rows, zero directory
  entries rendered across all of them
- PLAN-155: rendered `(no expected surface)`; spec section carries 14 directory entries
- PLAN-110: rendered 3 file entries; spec section carries 15 entries, 12 of them directories
- `epic-surface-partition classify`: independently resolves 15 and 14 entries for the same
  two specs

## The same blind spot sits in the disjointness check, where it matters more

`orchestrator corpus cross-check` — the verb whose output gates whether two plans may run
concurrently — shares the defect, and the consequence is worse than a misleading table.

Over the same 22-spec corpus it reports 20 file-overlap rows, and **PLAN-130, PLAN-140 and
PLAN-155 appear in none of them**. PLAN-130's Expected Surface claims `test/` — the entire
test tree — and its own spec text states it "is surface-incompatible with every other
`test/`-editing plan and must be sequenced, not paired." The check that exists to detect
exactly that reports it as overlapping with nothing.

So the two states the check can produce are not distinguishable at its output:

- a spec that genuinely overlaps nothing, and
- a spec that overlaps **everything** by claiming a directory or a recursive glob

Both render as absence from `file_overlap_matches[]`. The failure is fail-open in the
direction of pairing two colliding plans, and it is silent.

There is corroborating evidence that this has already been worked around by hand rather
than diagnosed: the epic's ledger carries a hand-maintained "disjointness notes the
generator cannot derive" section listing precisely the directory-level collisions the check
drops. The workaround was written; the reason it was necessary was never recorded, so every
consumer since has read a clean `cross-check` as evidence of disjointness.

**A caller must not read absence from `file_overlap_matches[]` as disjointness** until the
check resolves directory and glob claims, or reports per spec that it could not.

## Partial coverage update (plan implement-plan-04-gate-comparability)

The cross-check half above is now covered: `_contains` resolves `recursive_glob`
(`**`) and `directory` (`/`) containment with a `/`-boundary, and live plans with
no comparable surface are reported in `live_indeterminate_plans` with
`live_could_not_check_count` rather than contributing silence. The Ordered Queue
`Surface (expected)` renderer half remains open, so this lesson is retained for
that residue.
