title: review_commitments reconcile reports clear over a population that excludes every qgate self-review commitment
content: "## Problem

`plan-marshall:phase-6-finalize:review_commitments reconcile` exists to stop a simplify pass from deleting a line the review process committed to earlier in the SAME finalize run. Its own contract says two undetermined states fail CLOSED to a conflict — an untriaged `pending` finding, and a finding with a path but no line anchor (which binds its whole file).

Neither guard can engage for a self-review commitment, because the records never enter the population at all. `scripts/review_commitments.py:402` derives the entire commitment set from one query:

```python
read = query_findings(args.plan_id, finding_type='pr-comment')
```

`query_findings` without `--include-qgate` reads only the per-type plan-findings files. **Every `qgate` finding is outside that population by construction.**

Observed on plan `pre-push-gate-mutation-coherence` at HEAD `426982695`. That run made five commitments, all of them `qgate` findings (phase `6-finalize`, `source: qgate`, `type: bug`), all resolved `fixed`: `bea8e3`, `20d5ae`, `9e2c7e`, `1599c0`, `767d58`. Three were resolved by DELETING a claim and replacing it with a POINTER; two by REMOVING a cardinal from count-prose. Reconcile returned:

```text
verdict: clear   commitments_considered: 0   deletions_considered: 0   unanchored_commitments: 0
```

The `pr-comment` store was independently confirmed `findings_store_state: present`, `total_count: 0` — so the verb read a real, genuinely-empty store and correctly reported `clear` over it, while being blind to all five commitments it had been invoked to protect. A deletion reversing any of them would have returned `clear` just the same. What actually held the protection was the acting agent's own reading of the finding set.

## Why it matters

This is a **vacuous guard**: a confident `clear` whose denominator excludes the population the caller believed it covered. The archetype has recurred repeatedly in this corpus, and the standing rule it violates is the population-derivation one — *every set-guarding detector must be population-derived, and a check that can return 0 from an empty population MUST publish the population size.*

The verb does publish `commitments_considered: 0`, which is what made the defect visible here. But `verdict: clear` is the field a caller branches on, and nothing in the envelope marks the clear as vacuous. The `proves:` / `gates_merge:` discipline the verb already carries for its merge-verdict scope is exactly the shape missing for its population scope.

The exposure is not hypothetical: `finalize-step-simplify` runs at order 8, immediately after `pre-submission-self-review` at order 7. The step whose commitments most need protecting is the step that runs directly before the consumer, and it is the one class the population omits.

## Directive

1. **Widen the population to include qgate findings.** `derive_commitments` should read the qgate store alongside `pr-comment` — a self-review finding resolved `fixed` is a commitment in exactly the sense the verb means. Verify the flag name against `manage-findings` rather than assuming `--include-qgate` is the right lever.
2. **Make a vacuous clear legible in the envelope.** A `verdict: clear` computed over an empty population must say so in a field a caller branches on, not only in a count a caller may not read — mirroring the `proves:` / `gates_merge:` fields the verb already publishes. A clear over zero commitments and a clear over fifty are different answers and must not render identically.
3. **Regression test**: a plan with N `fixed` qgate findings and a diff deleting one of their anchored lines must return `conflict`, not `clear`.
