# Re-asking the same question of the same text is not independent verification - three self-review rounds re-derived one misreading before an external reviewer overturned it

## Context

During plan-170-make-the-attribution-usable's finalize, `pre-submission-self-review` ran five
rounds. Rounds 3, 4 and 5 each examined the same call site — `_read_baseline` in
`tools-epic-surface-partition/scripts/epic-surface-partition.py`, reading an operator-supplied
`--baseline-findings` path without guarding `OSError`/`UnicodeDecodeError` — and each round
dismissed it with the same reasoning: that the subcommand's documented exits-0-regardless
contract was "scoped to disagreements, not to an unreadable operator-supplied file".

CodeRabbit raised it on the PR. The unified triage read the shipped docstring rather than either
side's summary of it, and found: *"a disagreement is rendered, never raised, and the subcommand
exits 0 regardless — baseline drift included"*. `exits 0 regardless` takes no complement, and the
em-dash clause EXTENDS the scope to the baseline rather than confining it. The claim was
unconditional and the unguarded read falsified it. `--baseline-findings` was also the only
operator-supplied input not routed through the payload-error path.

Three consecutive dismissals were not three independent confirmations. They were one misreading,
re-derived.

## The mechanism

Each round re-asked *the same question* about *the same text* and, reasoning from a remembered
gloss of the docstring rather than re-reading it, reproduced the same error. Re-examination
produced no new signal because nothing about the second and third passes was independent of the
first: same reader class, same framing, same summary standing in for the source.

The corpus already covers two adjacent shapes and neither fits:
`2026-09-02-14-002` — each round clean on the *different* proposition it happened to ask.
`2026-09-02-14-001` — verify a delete-everywhere claim by re-searching, not enumerating.
This is the third: rounds converging on one answer because they shared an input, not because
the answer was corroborated.

## How to apply

When a review round DISMISSES a candidate that a prior round already dismissed, the repetition is
evidence of nothing on its own. Before recording the dismissal:

- Re-read the primary text the verdict rests on, in full, in that round. A remembered summary of a
  docstring, a contract clause or a spec sentence is not the text.
- State the dismissal as a quotation plus a reading, not as a conclusion. "The docstring says X,
  therefore Y" is falsifiable by the next reader; "this is scoped to disagreements" is not.
- Treat agreement with a prior round as a reason to check the shared input, not as corroboration.
  Independent confirmation requires an independent path to the answer.

An external reviewer disagreeing with a repeatedly-dismissed finding is a strong signal that the
dismissals shared a premise. Adjudicate on the source text; a prior dismissal is evidence to
weigh, never a precedent that binds.

## Why this matters here

The defect class the five rounds were hunting was precisely "a control passing green while a
sibling exposure stands" — a claim asserted more broadly than its evidence supports. The rounds
committed the same error in their own reasoning while looking for it in the code.
