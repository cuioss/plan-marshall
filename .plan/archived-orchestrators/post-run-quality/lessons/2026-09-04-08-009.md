# Bound the cost of re-firing finalize steps against the plan token anchor

**Carried out of the `review-apparatus` epic inbox** — message `review-packs-become-published-artifacts-007.md`, sender `review-packs-become-published-artifacts`, promoted by the orchestrator drain of 2026-09-04.

## Context

The plan consumed 4,063,723 tokens. Its calibration row is `multi_module + feature`, anchored at 1.5M warning / 2.5M error. The plan landed at **1.63x the error column**. Worked time was 154.3 minutes against the same row's 120-minute warning column.

6-finalize alone accounts for 1,944,394 tokens — 48% of the plan — across 26 configured steps. The step firing record shows: `pre-submission-self-review` 3 recorded firings after 2 failures (a 5-round convergence, 3 -> 4 -> 3 -> 1 -> 0 findings), `ci-verify` 3 firings, `automatic-review` 2, `sonar-roundtrip` 2, `project:finalize-step-plugin-doctor` 2, `push` 2, `adr-propose` 2, plus 2 loop-back iterations.

For comparison, 5-execute — where the actual implementation happened — consumed 620,880 tokens, under a third of finalize.

## Root cause

No finalize step carries a cumulative-spend ceiling, and no step's re-fire decision consults the plan's remaining budget against its anchor. A converging review loop is therefore unbounded in cost: each round is individually justified (the self-review really was still finding defects, and the loop really did converge to zero), but nothing observes that the aggregate has passed the error anchor.

The overrun is also invisible while it accrues. `metrics.md` renders the 6-finalize row as zero until an accumulator reconcile runs, and the anchor comparison happens only here, in the retrospective, after the plan has merged.

## Proposed action

Publish per-step firing counts and cumulative token spend against the plan's `(scope_estimate, change_type)` anchor as finalize proceeds, so the operator sees the budget position at each re-fire decision rather than afterwards.

Consider a soft gate: when cumulative spend crosses the warning anchor, a step's re-fire becomes an explicit operator decision rather than an automatic retry.

This is filed at medium confidence because the correct remedy is a design question — the loop converged and the defects it found were real, so simply capping the rounds would trade budget for quality. The finding is that the trade is currently made blind.

## Recurrence — 2026-09-07 (test-suite-anti-vacuity)

Recurred four times larger, and this time the uninformative fraction of the
re-fire cycle is measured rather than inferred.

### Context

`test-suite-anti-vacuity` (PR #1443, merged `33c8140f3`) consumed **16,371,072
tokens** against the `multi_module + tech_debt` row, anchored at 1.5M warning /
2.5M error — **6.5x the error column**, and the figure is a floor because the
`6-finalize` row never closed. Wall clock was 40h53m against the same row's
180-minute error column. 6-finalize took 9,507,115 tokens against 5-execute's
5,435,836, a **1.75:1 finalize-to-execute ratio** on a plan whose entire product
was 53 edited files in 22 tasks.

### What is new: the informative fraction of the re-fire cycle is now measured

The original lesson observed that the re-fire trade is made blind. This run
measures what the trade bought. Six head-bound finalize steps fired **44 times**
between them:

| Step | Firings | loop_back | Findings produced |
|------|--------:|----------:|------------------:|
| `ci-verify` | 9 | 0 | 0 |
| `pre-push-quality-gate` | 8 | 0 | 0 |
| `finalize-step-simplify` | 8 | 0 | 0 |
| `project:finalize-step-plugin-doctor` | 7 | 0 | 0 |
| `project:finalize-step-lessons-housekeeping` | 6 | 0 | 0 |
| `pre-submission-self-review` | 6 | 3 | 4 |

**41 of 44 head-bound firings returned green.** Every fix commit invalidates
`head_at_completion` and re-fires the whole set, so the informative yield of the
cycle is 3/44 = 7%. Only `pre-submission-self-review` ever went red; the other
five steps were green on all 38 of their firings.

The cost is not confined to dispatch tokens. `pyproject_build` alone owns
**50.3% of all script wall time** (11.65 hours across 407 calls) and
`tools-integration-ci` a further 17.8% — both driven by
`pre-push-quality-gate` and `ci-verify`, the two steps that never once turned
red.

### What this sharpens in the proposed action

The original remedy was a spend-vs-anchor display plus a soft gate at the
warning anchor. This run shows a cheaper and more targeted lever exists first:
the re-fire decision is **deterministic**, not a judgement. A step's verdict is
invalidated only when the intervening commits touch paths inside that step's
scope. A `refire | skip` verb comparing `head_at_completion` against HEAD and
intersecting the intervening changed paths with the step's declared scope would
have elided most of the 38 uninformative firings without weakening any gate —
no budget-for-quality trade at all, which is the objection that held the
original remedy at medium confidence.

## Recurrence — 2026-09-08 (pre-push-gate-mutation-coherence) — and a bound on the remedy

This recurrence is **counter-evidence for the proposed lever**, not a third
confirmation of it. It shows the anchor can be exceeded by a wide margin with
almost no re-firing at all, so a re-fire skip verb would not have helped here.

### Context

`pre-push-gate-mutation-coherence` (PR #1454, merged `f24b19a51`) consumed
**4,679,693 tokens** against the `single_module + bug_fix` row, anchored at 800K
warning / 1.3M error — **3.6x the error column**, and a floor, since `6-finalize`
never closed. Worked time 176.6 min against the same row's 90-minute error column.

The product was **8 files, 3 tasks, 2 deliverables** — the smallest plan yet to
carry this lesson, and by some distance.

### The distribution is the same; the mechanism is not

| Phase group | Tokens | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| planning (2-refine + 3-outline + 4-plan) | 1,525,322 | 32.6% |
| 5-execute | 567,102 | 12.1% |
| 6-finalize | 2,587,269 | 55.3% |

The finalize-to-execute ratio is **4.6:1**, higher than either prior recurrence.
But the re-fire population is nearly empty:

| Step | Firings | loop_back | Findings produced |
|------|--------:|----------:|------------------:|
| `pre-submission-self-review` | 3 | 2 | 5 |
| `ci-verify` | 2 | 0 | 0 |
| `automatic-review` | 2 | 0 | 7 (2 bot rounds) |
| every other finalize step | 1 | 0 | — |

`loop_back_iteration: 2`. Two of the four re-firings were *informative*
(self-review's loop-backs found 5 issues; automatic-review's second round found 2).
And the dispatch ledger is clean in both waste categories: `error_total_tokens: 0`,
`retryable_total_tokens: 0`, `unknown_count: 0`, across 15 boundary rows.

**So there was no waste to elide.** 55% of the run went to a single forward pass
over a 22-step finalize roster, at 11 dispatches averaging 235K tokens each. The
`refire | skip` verb proposed above would have saved at most two firings.

### What this bounds

The two prior recurrences both had a large uninformative re-fire population, which
made the re-fire lever look like the whole answer. It is not: on this plan the
**baseline cost of one clean finalize pass** is already 2.6M tokens, twice the
error anchor for the row, before any re-fire. Two consequences:

1. A re-fire skip verb is still worth building, but it addresses the *variance*, not
   the *floor*. It cannot bring a plan of this shape under its anchor.
2. Either the anchors for small-scope rows are calibrated against a finalize roster
   that no longer exists, or the roster itself needs a scope-proportional cut. The
   manifest here already pruned three steps (`finalize-step-security-audit`,
   `sonar-roundtrip`, `adr-propose`) under the `standard` posture and still ran 22.
   The `plan-efficiency` anchor table's `single_module + bug_fix` row is anchored,
   not `fallback`, so this is a real 3.6× miss against a calibrated value.

## Recurrence — 2026-09-13 (the-foreign-gate-population-and-branch-f-recovery) — the floor bound, confirmed on the same anchor row

This is the **fourth** datapoint and the second on the `single_module + bug_fix` row, which makes it
directly comparable to the `pre-push-gate-mutation-coherence` recurrence above. It confirms that
recurrence's bound rather than the earlier re-fire hypothesis.

### Context

`the-foreign-gate-population-and-branch-f-recovery` (PR #1473, merged `38af136ed`) consumed
**7,040,097 tokens** against `single_module + bug_fix`, anchored at 800K warning / 1.3M error —
**5.4x the error column**, and a floor, since `6-finalize` never closed and two phases recorded no
token measurement at all (`n=4/6`). Worked time 4h32m against the same row's 90-minute error column,
a 3.0x miss.

The product was **2 deliverables, 6 tasks, 28 landed files**.

So roughly 14 of 32 firings were uninformative — real, but nowhere near the 93% that made the
re-fire lever look decisive on `test-suite-anti-vacuity`.

### What this settles

The `pre-push-gate-mutation-coherence` recurrence argued the anchors miss on a *floor*, not a
*variance*, and proposed either re-calibrating small-scope rows or cutting the roster
scope-proportionally. This run is the stronger version of that same evidence:

- It is the **smallest-product plan** to carry this lesson by deliverable count (2), and it missed
  the error anchor by the **largest multiple** yet (5.4x).
- The two figures move in opposite directions across the four datapoints. A 53-file plan missed by
  6.5x, an 8-file plan by 3.6x, and now a 2-deliverable plan by 5.4x. Plan size does not predict the
  miss; the finalize roster does.
- Eliding *every* uninformative firing here would remove roughly 14 of 32 firings and still leave
  the plan far above 1.3M. The floor is the binding constraint on this row, confirmed twice.

The open question is no longer which lever to build first. It is whether the
`single_module + bug_fix` anchor (800K/1.3M) is calibrated against a finalize roster that still
exists. Four consecutive plans have missed their anchored row by 3.6x–6.5x; an anchor that no plan
meets is not measuring plans, it is measuring the roster.
