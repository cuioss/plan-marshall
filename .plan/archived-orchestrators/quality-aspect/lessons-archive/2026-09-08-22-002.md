# scope_creep_check emits an unregistered finding type and crashes at persist on every over-threshold plan

## The defect

`scope_creep_check` persists its finding with `type: scope_creep_warning`, which is not
a member of the `manage-findings` registered type set. The persist is rejected and the
guard exits 1:

```text
error: finding_persist_failed
message: "Invalid finding type: scope_creep_warning. Must be one of ('bug', 'improvement', ... 'pr-comment-overflow')"
```

The failure is **unconditional on the emitting path**: any plan whose residual exceeds
the threshold hits it. Below threshold the guard emits nothing and exits clean, so the
crash is invisible until the one moment the guard is supposed to speak. It has very
likely never successfully emitted a finding.

## Why this is worse than a crash

The guard's whole job is to say "this plan grew beyond what it declared". A plan that
genuinely crept gets `status: error` from the guard rather than a finding — and an
orchestrator reading only `status` sees a broken tool, not a scope warning. The signal
the guard exists to raise is exactly the signal it cannot deliver.

## Directive

Either register `scope_creep_warning` in the `manage-findings` type taxonomy, or emit
under an existing type (`improvement` or `anti-pattern` are the closest fits) with the
scope-creep discriminator carried in the finding body. Prefer whichever keeps the
retrospective queries able to isolate scope-creep findings from the rest.

Add a test that drives the guard **over** its threshold and asserts a finding is
actually persisted. The current tests evidently exercise only the under-threshold path,
which is why a type name that no registry accepts survived.

PLAN-155 (`close-the-runtime-slice-parametrization`), during a finalize loop-back at

the branch's own already-landed commits — not working-tree creep, so the guard was
