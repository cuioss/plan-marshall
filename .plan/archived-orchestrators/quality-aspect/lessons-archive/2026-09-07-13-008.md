# Two ways phase-5-execute reads an absent verdict as a passing one

## Context

Plan `plan-04-autonomy-gate-defaults` (epic `operator-ux`, PR #1437) produced two distinct 5-execute
defects that share one shape: **the absence of a verdict was consumed as a passing verdict.** They
are recorded together because the remedy is one contract change, not two.

**1. A live failure closed as already-fixed on an inverted read.** A failing test was dismissed as
already-fixed by reading pytest's assertion diff the wrong way round — expected taken for actual.
The test was failing at the time it was closed. (Q-Gate findings `1d9ff5`, `a57f9f`.)

**2. A timed-out focused build left three deliverables with no test verdict, and the run
continued.** The build did not fail; it timed out, producing no result. Three deliverables carried no
test outcome and execution proceeded as though verified. (Q-Gate finding `1d9ff5`, narration field.)

## Root cause

In both cases the consumer has two branches — passed and failed — and the real world supplied a
third: *no verdict*. An inverted diff read yields a verdict that does not correspond to the run; a
timeout yields no verdict at all. Both land on the permissive branch, because that is the branch an
absent failure falls into.

Defect 2 is the more dangerous of the two: it needs no misreading. A timeout is a normal operational
event, so the silent-pass path is reachable on any slow machine.

## Proposed action

1. A verification step reports **three** outcomes — passed, failed, and could-not-determine — with a
   timeout, a missing artifact and an unparseable result all mapping to the third. A deliverable
   carrying the third is not verified and must block phase advance or be recorded as unverified in
   the phase return.
2. When closing a finding as already-fixed on the strength of a test output, re-run the test rather
   than re-reading the captured diff. The re-run is cheap; the misread is silent.

## Impact

Three deliverables shipped in PR #1437 with no test verdict behind them. Nothing in the run said so
— the phase reported normally, and the landing reports 4/4 delivered. The gap is invisible to every
downstream consumer, including this ledger, which is why it is recorded here rather than left in the
run's own logs.
