# Evaluate main-anchoring the whole plan-locator path, not only its slot pre-check

A reviewer's architectural alternative to the sibling-worktree plan-read fix
shipped on PR #1489. It is **not a defect in the shipped behaviour** — the
deliverable's tests all pass and the fix does what it set out to do — but it
identifies a residual seam worth evaluating on its own terms.

## The suggestion

Finding `f1c087` (CodeRabbit, Major), against
`manage-status/scripts/_status_core.py` around line 922:

> Do not let the local slot pre-check suppress sibling resolution.
>
> `get_worktree_root()` uses the cwd-relative `get_base_dir()`. From worktree A,
> an absent A slot returns `_LOOKUP_NO_HOLDER` and skips `_run_locator()`, even
> when plan B exists in a sibling worktree.
>
> Removing the guard alone is insufficient. The locator's
> `resolve_plan_context()` channel and structural fallback also use cwd-relative
> paths. Make the locator path main-anchored, including this pre-check.

## Why it was accepted rather than fixed

The deliverable that shipped (D10 — "stop `manage-status`' plan read returning a
bare absence for a plan alive in a sibling worktree") closed the failure it was
scoped to close, and its tests demonstrate that. The reviewer is not reporting
that the shipped path is wrong; they are proposing a different and arguably
better anchoring model for the *whole* locator — the pre-check, the
`resolve_plan_context()` channel, and the structural fallback together.

That is a design change across three resolution channels, with its own
consumers and its own test surface. It is future-improvement territory, not a
bug in the PR that raised it, so it was resolved `accepted` and carried here.

## What a future plan should establish first

The suggestion asserts a concrete failure mode; before implementing, **verify
it** rather than assuming it. Specifically:

1. Does the cwd-relative `get_base_dir()` inside `get_worktree_root()` actually
   produce `_LOOKUP_NO_HOLDER` for a plan resident in a sibling worktree, at the
   shipped HEAD? Construct the two-worktree case and observe it — do not infer
   it from the code shape alone.
2. If it reproduces, is main-anchoring the whole locator the right remedy, or
   does it merely move the anchor problem? The reviewer explicitly notes that
   removing the pre-check guard alone is insufficient, which is the useful part
   of the observation.
3. Whatever is decided, the three channels must be anchored **consistently**.
   Anchoring one and leaving two cwd-relative is the state that makes an
   absence-versus-unreachable distinction unreadable, which is the defect class
   this plan was about in the first place.
