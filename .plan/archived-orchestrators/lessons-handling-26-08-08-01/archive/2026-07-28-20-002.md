# Vacuous guard: a guard whose predicate excludes its own motivating case

A guard can be individually sensible in every term and still be incapable of firing in the
scenario it was built for.

**Observed instance (API-Sheriff, PR #123).** A CI coverage step existed to make a PARTIAL
benchmark run visibly partial. It failed the job on `missing > 0 AND BENCHMARK_OUTCOME ==
success`. That predicate cannot fire in the motivating scenario: the suite goes partial
precisely BECAUSE Maven failed, so the outcome is never `success` when `missing > 0`. Both
conjuncts read as defensive care, so the contradiction passed review.

## The motivating-case substitution — apply when authoring OR reviewing any guard

1. State, in one sentence, the concrete scenario the guard exists to catch.
2. Assign the value each predicate term actually takes IN THAT SCENARIO.
3. Evaluate. If the result is false, the guard is vacuous — the guard and its motivating case
   are inconsistent.

## The specific smell

**A guard added in response to a FAILURE scenario that also conditions on a SUCCESS signal is
almost always vacuous.**

Ask additionally: *"in which scenario DOES this fire?"* If the answer is a residual case nobody
asked about, the guard solves a different problem than the one it was written for.

## Why this matters here

Vacuous guards are a recurring defect family in this repo (n=6 at time of filing), and at least
one was introduced BY A FIX for a previous one. Reviewers consistently read a conjunction of
individually-reasonable terms as thoroughness rather than checking whether the conjunction is
satisfiable. This procedure makes the check mechanical rather than a matter of noticing.

Related: every set-guarding detector must be population-derived — a predicate that can never
fire and a predicate over an empty population are the same defect wearing different clothes.
