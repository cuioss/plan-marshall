# Finalize re-firing dominates plan cost at 53% of all tokens

## Context

This plan shipped a 4-file change (2 production files, 2 test files; 254 insertions, 24 deletions) and consumed 3,622,870 tokens. The `6-finalize` phase alone accounted for 1,937,959 of them — 53%, more than execute (679,658), outline (430,651) and plan (419,700) combined. The `(scope_estimate, change_type)` anchor for `multi_module + tech_debt` sets its error column at 2.5M tokens and 180 minutes; the plan crossed both (3.62M tokens, 239 minutes wall). The token total is additionally a FLOOR, because `6-finalize` was never closed and its figure is a `(boundary floor)` fold of 14 dispatch-boundary rows, so true spend is higher still.

## Root cause

The cost is not in the code change; it is in finalize re-firing. Six finalize steps carry `firing_count` of 2 or 3 — `project:finalize-step-lessons-housekeeping` (3), `pre-push-quality-gate` (3), `project:finalize-step-plugin-doctor` (3), `pre-submission-self-review` (3), `finalize-step-simplify` (2), `ci-verify` (2), `automatic-review` (2), `branch-cleanup` (2) — roughly 24 firings across steps that would nominally fire once each. Two loop-back rounds drove this (`loop_back_iteration=2`): one productive (`pre-submission-self-review` returned findings), one forced by the merge-queue stall (`branch-cleanup`). Each re-entry re-runs expensive dispatched steps — quality gate, plugin-doctor, self-review — whose inputs did not change between rounds.

## Proposed action

Investigate whether a loop-back must re-fire steps whose HEAD-anchored inputs are unchanged. Several of these steps already record `head_at_completion`, and on this plan the three costliest re-fired steps all recorded the *same* SHA (`56d5d442`) across their later firings — evidence that the work was repeated against an identical tree. A re-entry check that skips a step whose `head_at_completion` still equals the live HEAD would have avoided most of the duplication. Note that the merge-queue-driven `branch-cleanup` loop-back (see the companion candidate lesson on merge-queue enqueue) is the avoidable trigger for one of the two rounds.

## Second occurrence — 2026-09-14, plan sweep-the-three-single-instance-defect-classes

Same shape, one plan later, and worse: `6-finalize` took **55%** of all tokens
(5,441,503 against `5-execute`'s 3,072,253) on a plan whose product is 87 mostly-trivial
test-file edits. **Finalize cost more than the work it was finalizing.** 27 finalize
dispatches, 6 `returned_with_findings`, `loop_back_iteration: 5`, and
`pre-submission-self-review` with `firing_count: 7` across two recorded loop-backs; four
other steps fired 4 times each.

### What the second occurrence adds: where the WALL time goes is a different answer

Tokens and wall time have different dominant terms, and the first occurrence only measured
tokens. Of 16,009,410 ms of script wall time, **68.1% sits in two CI-polling notations** —
`tools-integration-ci:ci` (32 calls, 5,457,300 ms) and
`phase-6-finalize:ci_complete_precondition` (26 calls, 5,447,910 ms) — against 24.2% in the
build wrapper. **The verification was cheap; the review and CI round-trips were not.**

### The remedy sharpens from "skip unchanged steps" to "bound the rounds"

The first occurrence proposed skipping a step whose `head_at_completion` still equals live
HEAD. That remains right and is not enough: nothing bounds the loop-back ROUND count, and
each round replays the full remaining step tail — another CI wait, another review-bot round.
Five rounds is a multiplicative cost. The `returned_with_findings` terminations are
productive in isolation, so no existing signal asks whether the **fifth** round still buys
enough to justify another tail.

Introduce a loop-back round budget for `6-finalize`, configurable like the existing finalize
budgets (`merge_queue_wait_budget_seconds`, `review_completion_poll_timeout_seconds` are the
precedent), and surface the accrued cost at each round boundary so spending another round is
a decision taken against a visible number. A plan running unattended has no other point at
which the cost becomes visible before the retrospective.

### Measurement caveat carried with this occurrence

Two sources disagree on this run's totals: `record-metrics` reports 9,380,546 tokens with
finalize 5,270,710 / execute 2,689,773, while the `plan_efficiency` aspect reports 9,882,263
with 5,441,503 / 3,072,253. The finalize-over-execute ratio is **1.96x** or **1.77x**
depending on the source. The conclusion is robust to the disagreement — both are far above
1.0 — but the disagreement itself is unexplained and should not be smoothed over.
