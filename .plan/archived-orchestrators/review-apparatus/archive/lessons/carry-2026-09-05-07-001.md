title: "false_positives_count conflates administrative rejections with wrong reviewer claims, and errs in both directions at once"
content: "## Rule

The review-retrospective derives `false_positives_count` from the `rejected`
disposition, but that bucket does two unrelated jobs. It holds administrative
non-findings — a bot's \"Review finished\" acknowledgement, a contentless
\"Actionable comments posted: N\" body, an incremental-comparison notice — and it
would also hold a genuinely wrong claim. The metric cannot tell them apart.

## Observation

Measured on PLAN `shipped-guards-assume-the-meta-projects-own-layout`, PR #1397:

- All **four** of CodeRabbit's `rejected` records were administrative. None was a
  wrong claim.
- The three premises that genuinely **were** wrong were every one dispositioned
  `taken_into_account`, and are therefore invisible to the metric:
  - a prescribed remedy that would have deleted this repository's whole-tree
    `test-compile` arm — the arm that exists because two type errors escaped to CI;
  - an anchor pointing at a line that already said what the reviewer asked for;
  - a suggested API surface that did not exist as described.

So the count **over-reports by four and under-reports by three simultaneously**. A
reader comparing reviewers on it is comparing noise, and the error is not even
directional — it cannot be corrected by scaling.

## How to apply

Derive the false-positive rate from a dedicated refuted-premise signal set at
triage time, rather than from a disposition bucket that also absorbs contentless
bot chatter. Failing that, exclude administrative comments from the denominator
before taking the ratio.

More generally: a metric whose input bucket serves two purposes reports neither.
Before publishing a per-reviewer comparison, check that each disposition feeding it
means exactly one thing."
