# Before treating a build timeout as budget-vs-code, check whether the preceding sync absorbed a dependency-graph change

## What happened

`pre-push-quality-gate`'s whole-tree arm returned `job_status=timeout` at 1197s against a resolved `bash_timeout_seconds=1226`.

⭐⭐ **The run refused to read that as either verdict, and that refusal is the first half of the rule:** a timeout is **not a red build and not a green one** — no verdict was reported, so there was nothing to triage and it must not be read as a pass.

Log inspection then established the cause was **budget, not code**: the build progressed normally to the last reactor module and was cut off mid-openrewrite pass, so it had **not stalled**. The worktree was confirmed clean after the timeout. The gate was re-run once per the documented timeout contract and completed in **540s**.

## The mechanism, and why it is predictable rather than unlucky

`finalize-step-sync-baseline` had pulled `cui-java-parent` 1.5.11 → 1.6.0, which forced *"Recompiling the module because of changed dependency"* across **every** module — a **cold full rebuild** — against a budget learned from **warm incremental runs**.

⛔ **Note the ordering that produces it:** `finalize-step-sync-baseline` absorbs upstream commits, and `pre-push-quality-gate` runs next **against a budget that has no knowledge of what was just absorbed.** The two are correlated **by construction**, not by chance.

## The rule

**Before treating a build timeout as a budget-vs-code question, check whether the immediately preceding sync absorbed a dependency-graph change** — a parent-POM version bump, a BOM bump, or any upstream absorb that invalidates every module's incremental state.

- **If it did** — the timeout is explained and a single re-run is the right response.
- **If it did not** — the budget itself is the subject and should be **escalated rather than re-run**.

## Generalisation

⭐ **An adaptive timeout learned from warm runs is systematically too small for the first cold run after a dependency-graph change.** The mismatch is not noise; it is a predictable consequence of the step ordering above.

A structural response worth weighing: have the adaptive budget **widen, or reset to a cold-run baseline**, when the preceding sync-baseline step reports an absorbed change to a parent POM or a dependency-management import, rather than continuing to predict from warm-run history.
