# phase-4-plan self-transitions to 5-execute, making the orchestrator's post-return q-gate-validation dispatch structurally unreachable

## Observed

Executing `/plan-marshall` for plan `always-on-is-not-a-resolve`, the phase-4-plan
dispatch completed and reported `qgate_validation_required: true` (the B2 surgical
bypass did not fire: `scope_estimate=single_module`, 6 declared paths). The
orchestrator then followed `plan-marshall:plan-marshall/workflow/planning-outline.md`
§ Step 4 → **Post-return q-gate-validation dispatch (conditional)** and dispatched
q-gate-validation with `activation_context: 4-plan`.

The validation dispatch refused:

```toon
status: blocked
error: phase_context_mismatch
requested_activation_context: 4-plan
plan_current_phase: 5-execute
phase_4_plan_status: done
```

No mutating step ran. The `module-mapping-validator` and `scope-criterion-validator`
checks the plan was entitled to therefore never executed, and the gap was silent
apart from the refusal itself.

## Root cause

Two documents disagree about who owns the `4-plan → 5-execute` transition.

- `plan-marshall:phase-4-plan` transitions the plan to `5-execute` itself, as the
  last action of its own dispatch.
- `planning-outline.md` § Step 4 places the q-gate-validation sibling dispatch
  **after** the phase-4-plan dispatch returns, and only then reaches its own
  Step 4b `manage-status transition --completed 4-plan`.

q-gate-validation enforces a **one-context invariant**: it is a sibling dispatch
belonging to the phase that is *currently executing*, never a replay against a
completed one. Because the phase self-transitions before returning, the window in
which `activation_context: 4-plan` is valid has already closed by the time the
orchestrator reaches the documented dispatch point. The dispatch is therefore not
merely mis-ordered on this run — it is **structurally unreachable on every run**
that gets as far as it, and Step 4b's transition call is a no-op re-assertion of a
transition the phase already made.

This is the same shape as the phase-2-refine self-transition noted in
`plan-marshall:plan-marshall` SKILL.md § Auto-Detect from Phase (the resume-time
metrics boundary reconciliation), where a phase skill self-transitioning silently
drops a paired orchestrator step. Here the dropped step is a whole validation pass
rather than a metrics stamp.

## Why it matters

The refusal is correct and fails closed, so nothing is corrupted — but the plan
loses a validation pass it was explicitly entitled to, and the loss is invisible
unless a reader notices `status: blocked` in a dispatch result. A caller that
treated the blocked return as benign would advance into `5-execute` believing the
task plan had been validated when it had not.

## Directive

Settle ownership of the `4-plan → 5-execute` transition in ONE place, then make the
other document consistent with it. Either:

1. **Phase keeps the transition** — move the q-gate-validation dispatch so it fires
   before the phase self-transitions, which means the phase must return a
   prompt-required-style signal and the orchestrator must dispatch validation while
   `4-plan` is still current; or
2. **Orchestrator owns the transition** — remove the self-transition from
   `plan-marshall:phase-4-plan` so `planning-outline.md` Step 4b is the sole
   transition site, leaving the post-return dispatch window genuinely open.

Option 2 matches the documented orchestrator flow as written and makes Step 4b's
existing transition call load-bearing rather than vestigial.

Whichever is chosen, check the same conflict at the sibling site: `planning.md`
§ Action: init documents an equivalent post-return q-gate-validation dispatch for
`phase-2-refine` (`activation_context: 2-refine`), and phase-2-refine also
self-transitions. Verify whether that dispatch is reachable before assuming this
defect is unique to phase-4-plan.

## Evidence

- Dispatch point: `plan-marshall:plan-marshall/workflow/planning-outline.md`

- Invariant enforced by: `plan-marshall:plan-marshall/workflow/q-gate-validation.md`
