title: A guard placed at an order where its producer has not run reports clear over a population that is empty by construction
content: "## Problem

A guard can be correct in every line of its own implementation and still be incapable of
firing, because it was **scheduled at a point in the lifecycle where its premise cannot
hold**. The population it reads is not merely empty on this run — it is empty *by
construction, for every run*, and the guard reports `clear`.

Two instances were verified first-party on plan
`shipped-guards-assume-the-meta-projects-own-layout`, in two different phases. They share
a shape but not a mechanism, which is why this is an archetype rather than a bug report.

### Site 1 — the producer runs 30 orders later (finding `1a1031`)

`phase-6-finalize/scripts/review_commitments.py` `cmd_reconcile` loads its commitment
population with `query_findings(plan_id, finding_type='pr-comment')` — pr-comment findings
only. Its sole caller is `default:finalize-step-simplify` at **order 9**. The producers of
pr-comment findings are `default:push` (11), `default:create-pr` (20) and
`plan-marshall:automatic-review` (~40).

No PR exists when simplify runs, so no review bot has commented, so no pr-comment finding
can have been filed. The commitment population is empty at that order **for every plan**.

Observed here as `verdict: clear`, `commitments_considered: 0`, `deletions_considered: 1`,
while six findings had already been resolved earlier in the same run with committed
resolutions, every one carrying a `file_path` (`8711b3`, `aaf3a4`, `0c4d54`, `0d0b79`
fixed; `344f61`, `93bf0f` taken_into_account). Those six are type `bug`, not `pr-comment`,
so the filter excludes them by design — which is the point. The guard's stated purpose is
to stop a simplify pass deleting a line the review process committed to, and at its own
call site the only commitments it can see are ones that do not exist yet.

### Site 2 — the workflow's own preceding step invalidates the invariant (finding `344f61`)

The Phase Entry Protocol orders Step 1 (Q-Gate check) **before**
`phase_handshake verify --phase 1-init --strict`. Step 1's documented job is to resolve
pending qgate findings tagged for the entering phase. Resolving one necessarily lowers the
live `pending_findings_blocking_count` below the value the previous phase captured, so
`verify --strict` reports drift every time a plan enters refine with a pending finding.

Concretely: phase-1-init captured `pending_findings_blocking_count=1`; refine Step 1
resolved finding `217976` as `taken_into_account`; verify then saw `observed=0` vs
`captured=1` and the leaf halted, correctly refusing to self-authorize an override.

Two properties make it a defect rather than a working guard: it is unconditional, so the
guard cannot distinguish a real regression from its own Step 1 doing exactly what the
workflow tells it to; and the invariant moved in the **safe** direction (fewer open
actionable findings), yet the gate treats a monotone improvement identically to a
regression. The cost is an operator authorization prompt plus a full re-dispatch of the
refine envelope on every such plan.

## Why it matters

Both guards are **honest at the field level and wrong at the placement level**.
`review_commitments` publishes `commitments_considered: 0`, so the emptiness is legible
rather than hidden, and it correctly declares `proves: removal_conflict_only` /
`gates_merge: false`. The phase-entry gate correctly halted rather than self-authorizing.
Neither guard lies. What neither can say is that the number it published was
*predetermined by where it was called from*.

That is the distinction this lesson exists to make, and it is why publishing the population
size — the remedy the corpus already prescribes for set-guarding detectors — is
**necessary but not sufficient**. A published `0` still reads as \"checked, nothing found\"
unless the reader independently knows the producer had not run. The defect is not a missing
field; it is an ordering claim nobody validated.

This is also distinct from lesson `2026-09-04-08-001`, which is the same *consequence*
reached by a different *mechanism*: there the composed manifest was out of ascending order,
so the runtime order disagreed with the declared order. Here the composer worked correctly
and the **declared order itself** is wrong relative to the producer. A fix for the composer
would not have caught either site above.

## Directive

- **When wiring a guard, name its producer and assert the ordering.** For any check that
  reads a population produced elsewhere in the lifecycle, state in the step's own doc which
  step produces that population and at what order, and require the guard's order to be
  strictly greater. An unstated producer order is an unvalidated assumption.
- **Distinguish `not-yet-produced` from `clear`.** A guard whose producer has not run should
  return a distinct third state, not the same verdict it returns after examining a
  genuinely empty population. `clear` and `nothing-to-check-yet` are different answers and
  a caller cannot recover the difference from a count.
- **Treat a monotone-safe movement as safe.** A drift invariant that fires on an
  improvement is measuring the wrong thing; make it one-sided, or re-stamp the capture
  after the workflow deliberately moves it.
- **Review trigger.** When a guard reports `clear` on its first live run, check whether its
  population could have been non-empty at that order before recording the pass.
