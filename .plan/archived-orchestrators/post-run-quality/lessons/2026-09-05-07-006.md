# Plan-level affected_files_recall hides a deliverable that shipped two thirds of its declared sites

## Context

The `artifact-consistency` aspect on plan `shipped-guards-assume-the-meta-projects-own-layout` reported:

```
affected_files_recall,pass,Recall 86% meets threshold
  declared: 21, found: 18, recall_pct: 85.7
```

A clean pass. But the recall is computed over the **union** of every deliverable's declared modification surface, and that union hides the distribution.

Deliverable 3 of this plan is titled *"Dispose of every residual shape-B site the partition names"*. It declared 9 paths under `Files expected to mutate:`. Six reached the realized footprint. Three did not:

- `marketplace/bundles/plan-marshall/README.md`
- `marketplace/bundles/plan-marshall/skills/marshall-steward/references/upgrade-flow.md`
- `marketplace/bundles/plan-marshall/skills/tools-script-executor/templates/execute-script.py.template`

That is 67% for the one deliverable whose title asserts completeness — below the 70% bar `request-result-alignment` uses to call a goal `fulfilled`. The other five deliverables shipped at 100%, and their volume carried the plan-level figure to 86%.

The gap was real and load-bearing: `upgrade-flow.md` needed an ad-hoc follow-up commit (`b217b8493`, branch `fix/upgrade-flow-content-drift-invocation`) immediately after this PR merged.

## Root cause

`affected_files_recall` measures one number over the whole plan. A deliverable that ships nothing is arithmetically indistinguishable from a broad plan that ships slightly less than declared, once other deliverables are in the denominator. The measurement is not wrong; its granularity is coarser than the claim it is used to check.

This bites hardest on exactly the deliverable shape that most needs checking — one whose title contains a completeness quantifier ("every", "all", "each"). Such a deliverable's value is entirely in its coverage, so a coverage measure that averages it against unrelated deliverables cannot check the thing that makes it worth doing.

`request-result-alignment` does compute per-goal coverage, but it is an LLM aspect with no threshold gate, so its `partial` verdict on deliverable 3 sits beside a `pass` from the deterministic aspect and does not contradict it.

## Proposed action

Have `check-artifact-consistency` emit `affected_files_recall` **per deliverable** as well as plan-wide, and fail the check when any single deliverable falls below the threshold even if the plan-level union clears it. Publish both figures with their own denominators, so a reader sees `plan 86% (18/21); worst deliverable 67% (6/9, D3)` rather than one averaged number.

Optionally escalate severity when the under-covering deliverable's title carries a completeness quantifier — that is the case where partial delivery contradicts the deliverable's own statement rather than merely under-delivering.

## Impact

A deliverable can ship two thirds of its declared surface, contradict its own completeness claim, require a same-day follow-up commit, and still leave the plan's deterministic coverage check green.
