title: await_fresh_review credits another bot's review as a per-bot completion signal
content: "## Problem

`github_re_review await_fresh_review --bot-kind {kind}` awaits a **specific** bot's fresh review. Its review-signal matcher does not filter candidate reviews by author, so **any** bot's review can satisfy the await for **any** `--bot-kind`.

Observed on PR #1454 (plan `pre-push-gate-mutation-coherence`, head `e488a7f8a`): the await issued with `--bot-kind sourcery` returned

```text
matched_review.user: coderabbitai[bot]
```

— a CodeRabbit review credited as sourcery's completion signal.

## Why it matters

The verb's whole purpose is per-bot participation proof. Crediting a foreign author's review inverts the property it exists to establish, and it fails in the direction that is hardest to notice: it reports **participation where there was none**, which reads as a settled arm and lets the run proceed.

That makes it a member of the confident-signal-hides-a-caveat family, and a particularly bad one because the corroborating machinery downstream is built on this signal. It also interacts badly with a real rate window: when one required bot is refusing or rate-limited and another is not, the refusing bot's arm can be satisfied by the answering bot's review — precisely the situation where a truthful per-bot verdict matters most.

The defect is invisible in the common case, because the bots usually all review and the matched author usually happens to be the right one. It surfaces only when the awaited bot did NOT review — which is the only case the await is there for.

## Directive

1. **Filter candidate reviews by author before matching**, mapping `--bot-kind` to its provider login (the same mapping the participation surface already uses to classify a comment's producer — reuse it rather than introducing a second mapping that can drift).
2. **Publish the matched author in the return** so a wrong match is legible at the call site rather than only in a transcript. `matched_review.user` is already present in the payload; the defect is that nothing compares it to the requested kind.
3. **Regression test**: an await for bot A, against a PR carrying a fresh review from bot B only, must NOT match — it must report the awaited bot as un-participated.

Check whether the sibling per-bot awaits (`bot_completion`, the rate-window claim path) share the matcher, and fix at the shared seam rather than at this one call site if so.

## Evidence

- `marketplace/bundles/plan-marshall/skills/workflow-integration-github/` — `github_re_review await_fresh_review`, the review-signal matcher
- PR #1454, the `--bot-kind sourcery` await returning `matched_review.user: coderabbitai[bot]`
- `marketplace/bundles/plan-marshall/skills/automatic-review/standards/bot-participation-contract.md` — the per-bot participation contract this signal feeds"
