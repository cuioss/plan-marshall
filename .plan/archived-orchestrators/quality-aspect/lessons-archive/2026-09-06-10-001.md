# An out-of-band loop-back override skips the re-arm the loop-back machinery owns

## Context

Finalize reached its loop-back ceiling at `loop_back_iteration=3` of `max_iterations=3`, and `automatic-review` then returned a fourth loop-back. The fourth was genuinely productive: the REQUIRED `cuioss-review-bot` was `participated_stale` at head `a7fa0c469` and its documented remedy is an explicit re-review trigger rather than a longer wait, and CodeRabbit had filed four new actionable findings at the same head. Halting would have merged with a required reviewer stale and four untriaged findings open.

The run proceeded, correctly, under the operator's standing instruction — logged at WARNING, and deliberately without mutating `max_iterations`, so the override stayed run-scoped rather than becoming a config change. The record is exemplary. The mechanism it had to work around is not.

Two consequences followed, and only the first is visible in the audit trail.

**First**, the ceiling counts productive and unproductive loop-backs identically. `manage-metrics` already distinguishes them — `returned_with_findings` is stamped precisely so "the dispatch examined its surface and filed findings" stays separable from `error` — and this run's `6-finalize` dispatch ledger carries 4 `returned_with_findings` rows against 9 `step_complete` and zero `error`. Every one of the four bought real detection. The admission gate cannot see that distinction, so a run whose review machinery is working well is throttled by the same counter as a run stuck in a fix-break cycle.

**Second, and undetected at the time**, the fourth cycle never went through `manage-status set-phase`, because it was not admitted as a loop-back at all — it was handled inside `automatic-review` under the override. The loop-back path is what re-arms the head-dependent finalize gates. Because it did not run, three gates carry verdicts anchored to a HEAD that is not the merged one:

| Step | `head_at_completion` | Merged head |
|---|---|---|
| `project:finalize-step-plugin-doctor` | `150ead51` | `c8cf10d5` |
| `pre-submission-self-review` | `150ead51` | `c8cf10d5` |
| `pre-push-quality-gate` | `b5400e55` | `c8cf10d5` |

`150ead51` is an ancestor of `b5400e55` (verified with `git merge-base --is-ancestor`), and two further change-sets landed after it. CodeRabbit's own second review body names the range `b5400e55..a7fa0c46` and lists `marketplace/bundles/plan-marshall/skills/build-pyproject/scripts/_pyproject_cmd_parse.py` among the files that changed in it — a script inside one of the two skill directories `plugin-doctor` had gated. A third change-set then landed between `a7fa0c46` and `c8cf10d5`, touching the same file again. So `plugin-doctor`'s recorded verdict, "plugin-doctor clean: 2 skills gated", was rendered over a version of `build-pyproject` that is not the one that merged, and `pre-submission-self-review` — the step that had already caught two real contract-drift defects in that same file's SKILL.md, including one introduced by its own earlier fix — never examined the final two rounds of edits to it.

Build and test coverage were not lost: `ci-verify` ran green at `c8cf10d5`, and both fix commits recorded a whole-tree verify. What was lost is the structural half — component quality gating and pre-submission self-review — and losing it silently is the part that matters, because nothing in `status.json` reads as a gap. Every step shows `outcome: done`.

## Root cause

The loop-back mechanism offers exactly two outcomes: halt at the ceiling, or an off-book override expressed in prose. There is no first-class way for an operator to authorise one more cycle *through* the machinery. Because the override necessarily routes around `set-phase`, it also routes around the re-arm bookkeeping that `set-phase` owns — so authorising the extra cycle silently buys a second, unasked-for effect: the head-dependent gates stop tracking HEAD.

The ceiling itself is also miscounted. It bounds total loop-backs when what it is trying to bound is unproductive churn, and the ledger already carries the discriminator (`returned_with_findings`) that would let it count the right thing.

## Proposed action

1. Give the admission gate a first-class **operator-authorised ceiling extension**: a recorded grant (reason + granting turn + a bounded increment) that the gate admits as a normal loop-back, so `set-phase` runs and the re-arm happens. The override then costs one field instead of an entire code path.
2. **Exclude `returned_with_findings` loop-backs from the ceiling count**, or give them a separate, higher budget. The counter should bound fix-break churn, not successful detection. `manage-metrics` already stamps the distinction; the gate need only read it.
3. Add a **pre-merge staleness assertion** independent of the loop-back path: before `branch-cleanup` authorises the merge, compare each completed head-dependent step's `head_at_completion` against the merge-candidate HEAD, and report every step whose anchor is an ancestor rather than an equal. A green step list at a stale anchor must not read as a green step list. This is the detector that would have caught the second consequence regardless of how the extra cycle was authorised, and it complements — rather than duplicates — lesson `2026-09-04-17-001`'s `verdict_inputs` proposal, which addresses currency comparison for steps that *do* re-fire.
