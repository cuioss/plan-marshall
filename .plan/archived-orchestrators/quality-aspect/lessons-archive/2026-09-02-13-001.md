# Apply the convergent resolution a self-review finding states, not a narrowing reword

## Context

`pre-submission-self-review` fired five times on this plan (`firing_count: 5`, four prior `failed`) and filed eleven findings, every one `defect_class contract_drift`, every one resolved by deletion in the end. The eleven were not evenly distributed across independent defects: rounds 3 and 4 filed seven of them, and at least three name an earlier round's own fix as their source.

- `45f9f0` and `977805` (round 3) both open with the literal words *"SELF-SEEDED by round 2"*.
- `276d72` (round 4) reads: *"Round 1 adopted this sentence as the 'true, narrower fact'; it is narrower but still false."*
- `ebbf9b` (round 4) reads: *"This is the round-1 clause with 'verdict' replaced by 'finding'; it was never swept out of the script."*

Round 1's own finding text (`7d498d`) had already stated the answer: *"Convergent fix is DELETION of the over-claiming clause; the analyzer docstring already states the true, narrower thing."* The fix narrowed the clause instead of deleting it, and round 4 filed the narrowed form as still false.

## Root cause

The round-loop termination rule in `phase-6-finalize/workflow/pre-submission-self-review.md` names self-seeding as the failure mode and prescribes deletion as the convergent resolution. The rule governs how a finding is *classified and reported*; nothing governs the *fix* applied between rounds. When a finding says "this prose claims more than the code delivers", a reword that claims slightly less is locally plausible and passes the author's own reading — and re-enters the next round's candidate set. Deletion is the only edit that cannot seed.

The finding text itself carried the correct instruction on the very first round. It was read and not followed, twice.

## Proposed action

Make the convergent-resolution instruction binding on the fix, not just on the report:

1. When a self-review finding's own `rationale` names DELETION as the convergent resolution, the loop-back fix MUST delete. A narrowing reword of the same clause is not an admissible fix for that finding and should re-open it.
2. Have the next round's candidate surfacer flag any candidate whose text was authored by a prior round of the same step (the `head_at_completion` anchors already scope each round's delta, so the provenance is recoverable) and report it as self-seeded *before* the round spends tokens re-deriving that classification by hand.

## Recurrence — 2026-09-02 (domain-over-provision)

Second observation, on an independent plan, with the same shape and the same terminal condition.

`pre-submission-self-review` ran **seven rounds** (`firing_count: 4`, three prior `failed`) and filed **25 findings**, every one `defect_class contract_drift` against documentation restating the `domain-detect` contract. Six of the 25 name a prior round's own fix as their source in their own text:

- `1d785c`, `05b908`, `f16140` (round 3) — *"SELF-SEEDED by round-2 fix 219b244d7"*
- `32b003` (round 3) — *"PARTIALLY SELF-SEEDED — round-2 fix 219b244d7 edited this sentence but left both defects"*
- `3ae5bb`, `8fd2af` (round 4) — *"round-3 residue: the round-3 deletion left … the same universal the code contradicts"*
- `dc2f31`, `192b91` (round 5) — *"SELF-SEEDED by 6c2d7b99d"*

The mechanism is identical to the original observation: each fix restated the contract in corrected words, and each restatement was independently falsifiable against the code. Round 3's finding `f16140` states the exit condition explicitly — *"Removing the ambiguous-predicate enumeration removes the class that has produced a finding in three consecutive rounds"* — and the class did stop seeding once every prescription became a pure DELETE plus an xref to one authoritative home. Rounds 6 and 7 filed findings against **pre-existing** documents, not against the previous round's prose.

This confirms rule 1 of the Proposed action on a second plan, and adds one qualification: obeying rule 1 terminates the *seeding* but not the *class*. See lesson `2026-09-02-14-001` for what round 6 then got wrong — a delete-everywhere fix whose completeness was verified by enumerating the sites the round already knew, rather than by re-searching the deleted literal over the inventory.

Rule 2 of the Proposed action remains unimplemented and would have paid here too: the self-seeded label was hand-authored into six finding bodies across three rounds, from inputs (a prior fix-commit SHA and a finding `file_path`) that are both already machine-readable.

## Recurrence — 2026-09-04 (domain-glob-seeding)

Third observation. The seeding is gone and the class is not — and this run isolates *why the class outlives the loop*.

Self-review fired twice and filed **4 findings, every one `defect_class contract_drift`**, every one resolved `fixed` by DELETE-and-xref, none self-seeded. Rule 1 was obeyed from round 1, so the loop terminated in two rounds instead of five or seven. That part worked.

What did not work is upstream of the loop. **All four findings are the same syntactic form — a CLOSED enumeration of an extensible set, restated away from its authority — and three of the four were already false at HEAD before this plan touched the file.** The change did not create the drift; it walked past it.

| Finding | The closed claim | Already wrong by |
|---------|------------------|------------------|
| `096505` | `_cmd_skill_domains.py:331` / `:295` — *"Only bundle reference and workflow_skill_extensions are stored in marshal.json"* | 1 key (`outline_skill`) before the change; 2 after |
| `abae44` | `extension-api/SKILL.md:112` — a Quick Reference table naming *four* optional hooks | 2 hooks before the change; 3 after |
| `5759c7` | `extension-contract.md:842` — *"`get_skill_domains` only, no other overrides"* as the minimal-bundle example | 2 overrides before the change; 3 after |
| `3c74bf` | `pm-dev-oci/.../extension.py:68` — *"One glob per entry of the `container_filenames` set"* | authored by this change (10 entries, 11 globs) |

Every prescribed fix converged on the same move rules 1 and 2 already teach — delete the restatement, point at the authority — and `abae44`'s own text spells the alternative out: *"delete the partial table and cross-reference extension-contract.md rather than maintaining a fourth copy."* Three copies had been maintained; the change updated two of them and missed the third, which is exactly the failure mode a fourth copy exists to produce.

**The new fact: a closed enumeration rots silently and no gate sees it until a change happens to touch the file.** Nothing in the repository fails when *"only X and Y"* becomes false — not a test, not `plugin-doctor`, not `quality-gate`. Self-review is the only detector, its window is the change's own delta, and its evidence here is that the tree carries an unknown number of already-false closed enumerations that will surface one at a time, years apart, charged to whichever unrelated plan next edits that file. Rules 1 and 2 make each *encounter* cheap; neither shortens the queue.

### Proposed action — rule 3 (prospective, authoring-time)

3. **When a change adds a member to an extensible set, treat the sibling restatements as part of the change's own surface, and delete rather than grow them.** Concretely, before the self-review round runs:
   - Search for the *set*, not the file: `architecture search --content` for the set's identifier (`container_filenames`, `workflow_skill_extensions`) and for the closure vocabulary that guards it (`only `, `no other`, and the spelled cardinals `three`/`four`/`five`). Escape regex metacharacters — see lesson `2026-08-25-09-011`.
   - A pre-existing wrong count found by that search is **in scope**, not someone else's debt. All three pre-existing defects above were filed against this plan and fixed by it; the only thing deferring them would have bought is a later plan paying more to rediscover them.
   - The admissible fix is deletion plus an xref to the one authoritative home — never incrementing the count, never appending the new member to the copy. Growing a copy re-arms it for the next member.
   - A count is the tell. Prose that says *how many* of an extensible thing there are ("four hooks", "one glob per entry", "only X and Y") is asserting a closure the set does not have, and is stale the day the set grows. Prefer a per-member claim or a cross-reference over any cardinal.
