title: "coderabbit refusal_patterns match the post-review window-spend notice, classifying a completed review as refused"
content: "## Problem

CodeRabbit's `refusal_patterns` match the **post-review availability notice**:

> Included review availability: … 0 remain after this review.

That line is attached to a **completed** review — it reports the window the review just consumed. The registry nonetheless placed `coderabbit` in `refused_bots[]` with `cause: quota` on a round where it had demonstrably reviewed the target commit and posted an actionable finding.

`refusal_pattern_drift[]` was empty, so nothing surfaced the mismatch.

## Why it did not bite here, and why it will

The participation predicate resolved it correctly — participation outranks a refusal observation, so `participated` won and no verdict was harmed. **The absorption is what makes it dangerous rather than harmless:** the misclassification is invisible in every round where the bot also participates, which is every round so far.

The case where it bites: a bot whose ONLY output in a round is that notice. It would classify as `refused` despite having reviewed — the reviewer's own success message read as its refusal. On a REQUIRED bot that is a false merge block; and because the same phrasing is what a genuine spend looks like after a real refusal, the two are indistinguishable to the pattern.

This is the \"confident signal hides a caveat\" shape at the registry layer: a pattern set that matches on the *presence* of quota vocabulary rather than on whether a review was produced.

## Directive

1. Narrow coderabbit's `refusal_patterns` so the post-review availability notice does NOT match. The discriminator is present in the same body — a completed review carries `Actionable comments posted: N` and a `final_review_risk_coverage` stamp; a refusal carries neither.
2. Prefer a POSITIVE test for refusal (the notice says no review was produced) over a keyword match on quota vocabulary, which a success message also contains.
3. Add a test pinning a real completed-review body that carries the availability notice, asserting it does NOT classify as refused.

## Evidence

PR #1427 round 4: body `13856a` stamped `e4da2226eb`, \"Actionable comments posted: 1\", `final_review_risk_coverage.kind: reviewed` — and `coderabbit` in `refused_bots[]` with `cause: quota` in the same producer output.
