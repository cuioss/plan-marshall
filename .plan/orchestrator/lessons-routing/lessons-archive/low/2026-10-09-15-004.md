# Stop counting subagent hand-backs as operator turns in chat extract-signal

## Context

The retrospective of plan `plan-lb-29-harness-sync` reduced the session transcript with `chat extract-signal`. The result reported `operator_turn_count: 67`, `gate_decision_count: 16`, 67 kept turns out of 2218, and a reduced text of 450,110 bytes. Reading the reduction, five of the 67 kept turns were typed by the operator (the launch command, `re-ask`, a shell line, `continue`, and `2`). The other 62 are final reports handed back by dispatched steps, which the transcript stores as user-role messages beginning "Another Claude session sent a message" and framed as a subagent hand-back. They make up nearly all of the 450 KB.

## Root cause

The operator-provenance test admits any user-role turn that is not empty and not a known harness injection. A subagent hand-back is user-role and carries a great deal of text, so it passes as operator-authored. The count that is meant to say how often the operator steered the run therefore measures how many dispatches returned.

## Proposed action

- Classify a user-role turn whose body is a subagent hand-back frame as its own kind, count it separately (for example `handback_turn_count`), and exclude it from `operator_turn_count` and from the `no_signal` decision.
- Decide deliberately whether hand-backs stay in the reduced text. They were useful evidence for this retrospective, but on a longer plan they would exhaust the 2 MiB read budget and turn the analysis into a size-driven skip. A bounded form (status block plus the first lines of each report) would keep the signal.
- Add a fixture with one typed turn, one gate decision and three hand-backs, asserting `operator_turn_count: 1`.

## Evidence

- aspect: chat_history_analysis — pre-pass figures: `raw_turn_count: 2218`, `reduced_turn_count: 67`, `operator_turn_count: 67`, `gate_decision_count: 16`, `reduced_transcript_delivered_bytes: 450110`; 5 operator-typed turns and 62 hand-backs observed in the reduced text.
- aspect: plan_efficiency — reading the 450 KB reduction was the largest single input of this retrospective.
