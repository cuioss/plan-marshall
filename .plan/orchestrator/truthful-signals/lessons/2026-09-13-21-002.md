# Phase-lifecycle entry protocol resolves Q-Gate findings before phase_handshake verify, making blocking-count drift reproducible at every entry

Observed twice while running plan architecture-store-query-truthfulness (PLAN-CIS-049) at phase-5-execute re-entry, once after a manual baseline-drift recovery loop-back and once immediately after authorizing the first override. The phase-5-execute Phase Entry Protocol runs its own Q-Gate entry-check step BEFORE phase_handshake verify --phase 4-plan --strict. When that entry-check resolves a pending actionable finding (here, be4ef7 -> taken_into_account), pending_findings_blocking_count (aggregated across ALL phases by _capture_pending_findings_blocking_count / _query_pending_qgate_count_aggregated in plan-marshall/scripts/_invariants.py) drops between the orchestrator captured value and the live value the very same entry observes moments later -- a self-inflicted, reduction-only drift with no other invariant changed and nothing regressed.

## Directive
This is a structural ordering property of the entry protocol, not a plan-specific anomaly: it will reproduce at every phase-5-execute (or other guarded) entry whose own entry-time Q-Gate step resolves at least one pending finding between the last captured handshake row and the verify that follows. When diagnosing a handshake drift whose ONLY diff is pending_findings_blocking_count moving DOWN by exactly the count of findings the entry protocol itself just resolved, treat it as this known ordering artifact -- verify the reduction is fully accounted for by entry-time resolutions (compare aggregate before/after against the specific findings resolved) and authorize an override with that evidence, rather than treating it as an unexplained regression. Consider fixing the root cause: either sequence phase_handshake verify BEFORE the entry-time Q-Gate resolution step, or exclude entry-protocol-resolved findings from the drift comparison, so a legitimate self-resolution stops manufacturing an override cycle at every entry.
