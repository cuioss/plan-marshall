# Fixing a producer contract without migrating its consumers leaves the defect alive where it matters most, while the plan reports it fixed

## The rule

When a deliverable changes a **producer's contract**, the plan must derive that producer's **consumer set** and migrate it in the same plan.

⛔ A consumer whose decision gates a **destructive or irreversible action** is in scope **by definition** — whatever the declared file list happens to say. Fixing the producer and leaving that consumer behind keeps the defect alive at exactly the point where it costs the most, **while the plan reports the defect fixed.**

## The instance

Plan `pr-065-settings-repo-accumulates-never-lands` (PR #1491), Q-Gate finding `9407ee` at 3-outline.

Deliverable 1 made `ci pr list` derive a complete population instead of reporting a page — the defect being that `gh`'s default page size was silently standing in for a count.

But `phase-6-finalize/standards/branch-cleanup.md` calls `pr list --head {branch} --state open` **with no `--limit`**, extracts a **bare count**, and that count drives the Safety Check that **ABORTS branch cleanup when other open PRs use the branch**.

⇒ After the producer landed, that call site would have received a `truncated` field it does not read and a default limit it does not set: **a page still read as a population, in the exact code path the deliverable existed to correct** — and the consumer in question is the one gating a **branch deletion**.

## What caught it, and what did not

⭐ The **scope-criterion validator** caught it, and the fix landed in the same plan (TASK-013).

⛔ **Nothing in the outline's own success criterion would have.** The deliverable could have satisfied every stated Done-when while leaving its most consequential consumer unmigrated — which is why "the producer is fixed" is not a completion test.

## The check to apply

Before closing a producer-contract deliverable, ask: *who reads this value, and what do they do with it?* Enumerate the call sites rather than the declared files. Rank them by consequence, not by proximity — and treat any consumer that deletes, merges, overwrites, or gates a transition as mandatory scope.
